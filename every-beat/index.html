    How to play

    1. One real animal a day, doing one thing: resting, diving, flying, hibernating.
    2. The heart in the middle starts at 60 beats a minute, about your own at rest. Drag up to make it faster and down to make it slower, or slide the scale on the right, from 1 to 2,000 beats a minute.
    3. When it feels right, tap Show its heart. The real heart appears beside yours, and you see how many times too fast or too slow you were.
    4. Tap the speaker to hear the beat. On some phones you feel it too.

    One heart a day, the same for everyone. A new one arrives at midnight.

    Where the figures come from

    Each heart rate was measured by scientists, vets or doctors, and was read on two websites; both are linked under the answer. A heart's pace moves from minute to minute, so anything within about 15% of the real figure counts as spot on.

    Fast hearts

    Nothing on this page flashes, and nothing pulses more than three times a second. A heart that beats faster than that holds still, and the line underneath shows its pace instead.

    The pictures

    The silhouettes come from PhyloPic, a free library of drawings of living things. Artists who ask for credit are named under the answer. PhyloPic does not endorse this site.
