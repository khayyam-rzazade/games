THE IDEA TO CHECK (working name "False Friends", for the site Logicers, logicers.com)
  A party game on ONE phone (or laptop) for 2 to 8 people at the same table.
  - The phone shows a word that looks (almost) the same in two languages, for example "gift: English and
    German", "burro: Spanish and Italian", "barn: Swedish and English".
  - Question: does it mean the same thing in both languages (a "true friend") or something different
    (a "false friend": German "Gift" means poison; Swedish "barn" means children)?
  - Twists being considered: the player holding the phone reads out loud a short sentence that uses the word
    in the other language, in their best accent; the table talks it over, using the sentence as a clue;
    then everyone votes at the same moment (thumbs up = same meaning, thumbs down = false friend); then the
    phone reveals both meanings, the sentence translated, a funny line of what the sentence would say if you
    mixed the two meanings up, and two dictionaries as sources. A point for each right vote; the phone goes
    to the next player for every word; about ten words a game.
  - About half true friends and half false friends; many language pairs (English with German, French,
    Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish; pairs without English such as Spanish-Italian,
    Spanish-Portuguese; Russian, Turkish, Azerbaijani).

  What would make it THE SAME GAME: a game (app, web game, board or card game, TV or radio format,
  classroom game) in which a GROUP is shown a word that looks the same in two languages and must decide
  whether the meaning is the same (true friend) or different (false friend), with both meanings shown after.
  Say exactly which of these parts each find has and lacks:
    A = played by a group at a party or a table (not one learner alone)
    B = a word that looks the same in two languages
    C = the question "same meaning or false friend?" (true friend vs false friend)
    D = a reveal with both meanings (and sources)
    E = many language pairs (not just one)
    F = one device passed round the group
    G = a sentence read out loud, or the meaning guessed from a sentence in context
    H = everyone votes at once, with points

  A = played by a group at a party or a table (not one learner alone)
  B = a word that looks the same in two languages
  C = the question "same meaning or false friend?" (true friend vs false friend)

  F = one device passed round the group
  G = a sentence read out loud, or the meaning guessed from a sentence in context

PART 6: BOTTOM LINE FOR MY AREA
-------------------------------
- SAME: none found.
- The idea's core (a group judging whether a lookalike word is a true or false friend) exists only as
  small classroom activities for one language pair (teachit, tes). It does not exist as a published
  party, card or board game.
- The nearest published games, the two "Lost in Translation" games (2018 and 2024), show there is a
  market for table games about foreign meanings with about ten cards a game. But they use idioms, not
  lookalike words, and they have no true/false friend vote.
