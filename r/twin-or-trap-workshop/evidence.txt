THE RULES FOR A PAIR (Khayyam's brief; how they were applied: Claude, marked C)
  1. Two words that look the same in two languages: the same letters apart from capitals and accents, or, for
     Russian, nearly the same letters in Latin transliteration (фабрика, fabrika; English fabric).
  2. Every word read in two dictionaries, each page opened and read, the quotes here. English: two of
     Merriam-Webster, Oxford Learner's and the Britannica Dictionary (the Merriam-Webster page for learners counts
     as Merriam-Webster). Other languages: two established dictionaries where they could be read, or one and a
     second dictionary of the same publisher (Larousse and Larousse Collège; DWDS and WAHRIG or WDG, which DWDS
     hosts), or one and English Wiktionary or the French Wiktionnaire. A bilingual dictionary counts (SpanishDict,
     Tureng, Zargan, synonymer.se, Woxikon).
  3. TWIN: the two words share their meanings. TRAP: no current meaning in common. A half case, a word that is a
     false friend in only some of its meanings, is left out.
  4. Which senses count (C): a sense the dictionary marks as old ("veraltet", "vieilli", "obsolete", "archaic")
     does not count. A sense marked slang, jargon or technical, a regional sense, or a figure of speech built on
     the main sense, does not decide a pair, unless it is a common use. But a TWIN must not have a common sense on
     one side only, and a TRAP must not have any shared sense, however small, that a player might know.
  5. Family-friendly (C): a pair is left out if a word's common use is rude, vulgar, sexual, about drugs, an
     insult, or about religion, ethnicity or politics. The game shows one checked meaning per word and never the
     others. Senses of that kind that a dictionary lists far down, marked slang or old, are named here so that
     nothing is hidden:
       English "glass": Merriam-Webster sense 5, crystal meth (slang).
       French "four": Wiktionnaire, argot, a place where drugs are sold; "four crématoire"; also an adjective and
         noun about the Fur people of Darfur and their language (a different word, same spelling).
       French "parachute": Wiktionnaire sense 8, drug slang.
       English "son": Oxford sense 5 and Merriam-Webster sense 2, Jesus Christ as the second person of the Trinity.
       English "estate": Merriam-Webster sense 3, a social or political class (the old estates: nobility, clergy,
         commons).
     None of these is shown by the game or touches the meaning it shows.
  6. Meanings and sentences: in Claude's own words, short and plain, showing only the checked meaning; never
     copied from a dictionary. The dictionaries are linked as the sources.

PART B: THE BRIEF FOR THE DICTIONARY HELPERS, AND THEIR LISTS

BRIEF FOR THE DICTIONARY HELPERS (6 Oct 2026, late evening)
===========================================================

WHAT THIS IS FOR
  A party game, "Twin or Trap", for the site Logicers (logicers.com). The phone shows a word that looks the same
  in two languages. The players vote: does it mean the same in both (TWIN, a "true friend") or something
  different (TRAP, a "false friend")? Every meaning the game shows must be checked in TWO dictionaries, each
  page opened and read, with word-for-word quotes kept as evidence.

YOUR TASK
  For each pair in your list, find and read TWO dictionary entries for EACH of the two words (so usually four
  entries per pair), and report EVERY current sense each dictionary gives for that word, word for word, with
  its labels.

  Why every sense: the game leaves out "half cases". A pair is a TWIN only if the two words share their
  meanings; a TRAP only if NO meaning of one word is a meaning of the other. If one word has a sense that the
  other also has (for a trap), or one word has a common sense that the other lacks (for a twin), the pair is
  a half case and is left out. So I need ALL senses with their labels (archaic, obsolete, dated, informal,
  slang, regional, technical, vulgar). Also report a sense that is rude, sexual, about drugs, alcohol,
  religion, ethnicity or politics: such words are dropped.

DICTIONARIES THE PAGE READER CAN READ (tested tonight)
  English:    merriam-webster.com, oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com, britannica.com/dictionary
  German:     dwds.de  (duden.de/rechtschreibung/... pages: untested, try if they come up)
  Dutch:      woorden.org  (also try: nl.wiktionary.org, ensie.nl)
  French:     larousse.fr  (also try: cnrtl.fr, fr.wiktionary.org)
  Spanish:    dle.rae.es, rae.es/diccionario-estudiante, spanishdict.com
  Italian:    treccani.it/vocabolario  (also try: dizionario.internazionale.it, garzantilinguistica.it)
  Portuguese: dicionario.priberam.org, infopedia.pt
  Swedish:    synonymer.se (Swedish-English lexicon)  (also try: sv.wiktionary.org, tyda.se, bab.la, folkets-lexikon)
  Russian:    dic.academic.ru (Ozhegov's dictionary), gramota.ru
  Turkish:    tureng.com (Turkish-English)  (also try: sozluk.gov.tr)
  Any language: en.wiktionary.org (one page holds sections for many languages under one spelling)
  CANNOT be read (do not use): dictionary.cambridge.org and collinsdictionary.com (403), dictionnaire.lerobert.com
  (403), wordreference.com (robots), svenska.se (no content), vandale.nl (free dictionary closed),
  obastan.com (unreachable), tr.wiktionary.org (cannot be fetched).

WHICH TWO DICTIONARIES
  - English words: two of Merriam-Webster, Oxford Learner's, Britannica Dictionary (not Wiktionary).
  - Other languages: if possible two established dictionaries (the lists above); otherwise ONE established
    dictionary plus English Wiktionary. Never two Wiktionaries for the same word. A bilingual dictionary
    (Spanish-English SpanishDict, Turkish-English Tureng, Swedish-English synonymer.se) counts as a dictionary.

HOW TO SEARCH (searches are scarce)
  One search per pair, limited with allowed_domains to the dictionary sites of BOTH languages, for example for
  German "Hut" and English "hut": query "Hut hut meaning", allowed_domains ["merriam-webster.com",
  "oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com", "dwds.de", "en.wiktionary.org"]. The results usually hold an entry page of
  each site. If one language's entry is missing, ONE more search limited to that language's dictionaries.
  For words with different spellings (Russian), one search per word.

RULES (strict)
  1. You have a FIXED number of web searches (stated in your task). Count them. Never go over. A refused
     search counts too. Use WebSearch with mode "standard". Load the tools first with ToolSearch (query
     "select:WebSearch,WebFetch").
  2. Open a page (WebFetch) ONLY if its exact address appeared in YOUR OWN search results in this task, or is
     a link you copied from a page you opened. NEVER type an address from memory or guess one. If a page
     fails (403/404/blocked), note it and move on; do not try other addresses for it.
  3. Do not use the Workflow tool. Do not start other agents.
  4. Ask the page reader for the senses word for word, e.g. prompt: "Quote word for word every numbered sense
     of the [language] word 'X' on this page, with its part of speech and any labels (archaic, informal,
     slang, regional, technical). Do not summarize." Put quotes in "double quotes"; mark cuts with [...].
     If the reader summarized or translated instead of quoting, write "(reader's summary)".
  5. For each word say plainly whether any of its senses means what the other word means, e.g. "Does any
     sense of German 'Hut' mean a small simple building (English 'hut')? No."
  6. Write your report into your file AS YOU GO (append after each pair), so nothing is lost. Format:

     === PAIR <id>: <language A> "<word A>" / <language B> "<word B>" ===
     <language A> "<word A>":
       D1: <dictionary name> | <address opened>
           senses: 1. "..." 2. "..." (labels)
       D2: <dictionary name> | <address opened>
           senses: ...
       Shares a sense with <language B> "<word B>"? yes/no: which
     <language B> "<word B>": (the same)
     HELPER'S VIEW: TWIN / TRAP / HALF / UNCLEAR: one line why
     Searches used so far: N of M

  7. At the end reply with a short summary (under 200 words): pairs done, which look HALF or UNCLEAR, which
     dictionaries failed, searches used.
