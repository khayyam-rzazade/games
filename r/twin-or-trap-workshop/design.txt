TWIN OR TRAP: how the game is made (night of 6 to 7 Oct 2026)
==============================================================

What it is
  A game for one phone and two to eight players, the fifth game for groups ("Pass the phone" on the home page) and
  the first that is not about countries. Working name "False Friends" (group-games.md 1.7, idea I1 of the long list).
  Ten words a game. Each looks the same in two languages: a TWIN means the same in both (a true friend), a TRAP
  means something else (a false friend: German "Gift" is poison). The player holding the phone reads out the word
  and its sentence in the other language, the table talks it over, and on three everyone votes at once with a thumb:
  up for twin, down for trap. The answer shows both meanings, the sentences in English and, for a trap, how the
  sentence sounds to the other language's ear ("To an English ear: 'These berries are a gift.'"), with the two
  dictionaries of every word. The holder taps who was right; right scores 1 point, right while most of the table
  was wrong scores 2. Then the phone goes to the next player. Most points after ten words wins.
  Not daily: no day number, streak or album. Everything below marked (C) in the plan was decided by Claude by
  Khayyam's five rules (simple, attractive, tempting to share, easy to play, teaches something).

The twist (C): why the table talks and laughs
  The idea of 1.7 was a vote by thumbs with little talk: for anyone who does not know the language, a coin toss.
  Three changes make the table talk and laugh, without a rule that takes more than a sentence:
  1. Read it out. The holder reads the word AND a short sentence with it, in the other language, accent and all.
     Reading a sentence in a language you do not know is funny in itself, and the sentence is a clue: "Il cane abbaia
     al postino" - does a walking stick bark? "Tengo once primos" - "I have once cousins"? The table decodes it
     together, from the words it recognises. Twins and traps both have sentences, so the clue never gives the answer
     away by itself; it rewards reasoning (the site is Logicers).
  2. Talk it over, then all vote at once. Nobody can copy, but everybody can argue first.
  3. Right against the table scores 2. Being right while most of the table was wrong doubles the point. So it pays to
     argue ("trust me, it's a twin") and to hold your ground against the room; a lone right thumb is the moment the
     table remembers.
  The laugh at the reveal: the mix-up line, the sentence as it would sound to the other ear ("Would you like a room
  in your coffee?", "Bread and donkey, please."). Not a fourth rule: it is just there to be read out.
  Considered and left out (C): the holder sees the answer first and bluffs (that is So-Called Expert's game); the
  holder makes up a sentence (stressful for shy players, and the phone cannot check it); a question about the real
  meaning with four choices (a quiz, less talk); a language setting (below).

The start, in ten seconds
  "Same word. Same meaning?" and "Each word looks the same in two languages. A twin means the same in both; a trap
  means something else. Read out the word and its sentence, talk it over, then all vote at once. Right scores 1
  point, or 2 if most of you got it wrong." Players 2 to 8 with - and + (3 at first), "Names" (optional: a window with
  one field per player), Start, and "2 to 8 players · one phone · ten words · about 10 minutes".

A word
  1. Passing the phone (as in Still In, So-Called Expert and Beat the Phone; Khayyam, 5 Oct 2026, late evening): a
     whole screen in heather says what the last word gave ("A new game: ten words, each in two languages.", "Trap! In
     German, bald means soon. Only Bo was right: 2 points." or "Twin! In German and English, Rucksack means the same.
     Everyone was right: 1 point each."), a phone with an arrow, "Pass the phone to" and the player's name in big
     letters, "Word 3 of 10. Bo reads it out.", and the button "Bo has the phone", which that player taps. Under it:
     "Word 3 of 10 · Scores" (a window with the points so far).
  2. The word: "Word 3 of 10 · Bo reads it out"; a card like a dictionary's entry with two rows, the language in small
     capitals and the word in a book typeface (Literata): "GERMAN Gift" and "ENGLISH gift" (for Russian also the word
     in Latin letters: "фабрика fabrika"); "In German:" and the sentence in italics, the word in it upright and in the
     game's colour ("Diese Beeren sind Gift."; for Russian also in Latin letters); "Twin or trap?"; "Talk it over. Then,
     on three, everyone votes at once: thumbs up for twin, thumbs down for trap."; the button "Show the answer".
  3. The answer: a panel in heather with "Word 3 of 10 · German and English", a stamp "TRAP!" or "TWIN!", and the
     meanings ("German Gift: poison", "English gift: a present (or a talent)"; for a twin one line: "German Rucksack and
     English rucksack: both: a bag you carry on your back"); then every sentence with its English ("Diese Beeren sind
     Gift." / "These berries are poison."), and under the sentence it belongs to the mix-up line ("To an English ear:
     'These berries are a gift.'"); then "Dictionaries German: DWDS · Wiktionary. English: Merriam-Webster ·
     Britannica Dictionary." (each a link); the button "Who said trap?" (or "Who said twin?").
  4. Who was right: "It's a trap. Who said trap?", "Tap everyone who voted trap. Right scores 1 point; if most of the
     table got it wrong, 2.", one button per player (pressed or not), and "Score it" (or "Nobody said trap",
     "Everyone said trap"); under it the points so far. Then the phone goes to the next player, or after the tenth
     word to the end.
  5. The end: "Ana wins!" (or "Ana and Bo share the win!", "3 players share the win!", "Everyone shares the win!"),
     "With 12 points, after ten words.", every player's points and "7 of 10 right, 2 of them for 2 points", the wins
     at this table, "Play again" (the same players and their wins; the next player reads first), "Share", "All ten
     words" (a window: twin or trap, both words and meanings, how many were right) and "New players" (clears the names
     and the wins, as Restart does).

The rules, and why (C)
  - Points: 1 for each player who was right; 2 each when they were fewer than half of the table (with two players a
    lone right player is half, so 1). Most points wins; a tie shares the win. No rounds, no target score.
  - Ten words a game: about ten minutes, and every player reads at least once with up to eight players (two or more
    times with fewer).
  - Four, five or six twins in a game, at random (each about a third of the games): about half twins, as 1.7 asks,
    without a fixed count that a table could use ("five traps gone, so the rest are twins").
  - No language setting (1.7 proposed one): with 67 pairs a table that keeps only its languages would get one or two
    games before the words repeat, and the setting would be one more choice before the fun. Every game mixes the
    languages instead: at most three words of one language (English apart), and one language twice in a row only
    where it cannot be helped. So a mixed table always has something for everyone.
  - Fresh words first: the last 40 words a table had come back only when no fresh word of their kind (twin or trap)
    is left; then the oldest first.
  - Which sentence is read out: the one in the first word's language (for pairs with English, the other language).
    Pairs without English have one sentence in each language; both show in the answer.
  - The holder taps who was right after the reveal (as in So-Called Expert): the thumbs were shown at once, so the
    holder saw them.
  - 2 to 8 players: two can play (the bonus then never applies); eight, as in the other games for groups.

The stock (see evidence.txt and build.py)
  67 pairs: 30 twins and 37 traps (45% twins), in ten languages: English with German, Dutch, French, Spanish, Italian,
  Portuguese, Swedish, Russian and Turkish, and 13 pairs without English (German with Dutch; French with Spanish;
  Spanish with Italian and with Portuguese). No Azerbaijani pair: no Azerbaijani dictionary gave two readable entries.
  Every meaning was read in two dictionaries, each page opened and read, the quotes in evidence.txt; build.py checks
  every rule and writes data/twin-or-trap.js and words.csv; test/test_build.py breaks each rule once (28 cases).
  The half-case rule as applied (C): a twin shares its meanings; a trap shares none, however small; senses marked
  old do not count, and a sense marked slang, jargon or technical decides nothing unless it is a common use. Words with
  a common rude, drug, insulting or sensitive use are left out (evidence.txt, part A, names every such sense that was
  kept far down a dictionary's list, and every pair left out with its reason).
  The meanings, sentences, translations and mix-up lines are Logicers' own words, written to show only the checked
  meaning; the dictionaries are linked.

Name, colour, typeface (C)
  Name: Twin or Trap. "False Friends" is taken (a Google Play quiz app, an itch.io game, classroom games), "Friend or
  Faux" is a party card game, "Word Twins" is near WordTwin (an iPhone synonym game), "Doppelwords" near Doppel-Gengo
  (a Japanese homonym board game). "Twin or Trap" was found nowhere; the nearest in words is "Truth or Trap", a trivia
  card game. It says the two answers, it alliterates, and the share line can ask it.
  Colour: heather, #845B83 by day (tint #F5E6F4), #C29BBB at night (tint #190D18). No other game uses or has reserved
  it. In OKLab 0.117 from mocha, 0.118 from magenta and 0.125 from plum by day, 0.099 from violet at night; the closest
  pair of existing games (teal and green) lie 0.059 apart. White text on it: 5.52 to 1; on its tint 4.6 to 1.
  Typeface for the words and sentences of other languages: Literata (SIL Open Font License; fontsource 5.3.0), three
  subsets (Latin, Latin Extended, Cyrillic) in two styles (600 upright for the words, 400 italic for the sentences):
  six files, about 100 KB, loaded only by this page and only for the letters on screen. The site's own typefaces have
  no Cyrillic and no ə (Azerbaijani): Literata has both, and ı, ş, ğ for Turkish.

Sharing (C)
  The picture (1080 by 1350, heather): "Same word, two languages:", a white card with the word that fooled the most
  players, in its two languages, "Twin or trap?", "It fooled 3 of our 4" (or "all 4 of us", or "Nobody at our table
  was fooled"), "Ten words that look the same in two languages. Does each mean the same?", "Can your table tell?" and
  the address. Never the answer. The message: "Twin or Trap: Gift in German and gift in English: same meaning or not?
  It fooled 3 of our 4. Can your table tell twins from traps?" and the link (?w=the ten ids&k=the word&f=fooled&n=
  players), which deals the same ten words in the same order to a friend's table ("A friend's table played these ten
  words. Gift (German and English) fooled 3 of their 4. Can your table tell twins from traps?", "Play their words").

Starting afresh, and what is kept in the browser
  As every game for groups since 6 Oct 2026 (Khayyam, evening; the plan, section 2): every opening of the page (from
  the home page, the Back button, a reload, or a page the browser brings back from its memory) starts afresh with three
  players and no names. The number of players, their names (optional, cleaned, at most 14 letters), the wins per name,
  who reads first next time and the game going on live only while the page is open, and are never stored. A round
  button in the bar (named "Restart the game"), only while a game is in play, asks "Restart the game?" ("Everything
  starts over: this game, the players' names and the wins at this table.") with Restart and Keep playing; "New
  players" at the end does the same; "Play again" keeps the players and their wins. A pull on the page does not reload
  it (overscroll-behavior). For the checks the page has a hook: TwinOrTrap.load(players, game) and TwinOrTrap.now().
  The browser keeps under "groups" > "twin-or-trap" only the number of games and the last 40 words. Nothing under the
  daily games; nothing counts in the Today card, the streak or the returning-player events. The privacy line: "The
  players' names and wins are forgotten when you leave this page."
  (This chat first built the game with names, wins and a game in progress kept in the browser, as the games before it
  did; it found the new rule when it read the plan again before its full run, and changed the game to it.)

Counter events: twin-or-trap/started, /finished, /share, /challenge-opened; none names a word or a player.

Weak spots, plainly
  - Someone who speaks one of the languages knows the answer at once; the bonus for being right against the table
    makes that a duel of knowledge and nerve rather than a dead round, but a table of linguists will find it easy.
  - Some sentences give a trap away to a careful reader (a chandelier "on the table"); that is the reasoning the game
    wants, but it makes some words easy.
  - 67 words last about six games before a table meets one again.
  - English is in 54 of the 67 pairs; Russian has one pair, Turkish six, Azerbaijani none.
  - Nobody but Claude has played it.
