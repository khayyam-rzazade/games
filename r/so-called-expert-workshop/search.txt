THE IDEA TO CHECK (working name "Expert Interview", for the site Logicers)
  A party game on ONE phone for 3 to 8 people in the same room.
  - Each round one player is "our expert" on a country (players take turns).
  - The phone secretly shows only the expert a card: a country and five short facts about it.
    Four are TRUE real-world facts (each with sources). ONE is INVENTED by the game, and the card marks it
    for the expert only ("This one is invented. Say it like the others.").
  - The expert presents the country for about a minute, in their own words, like an expert on TV, and works
    all five facts in. The others may ask up to three questions; the expert answers in character.
  - Then everyone else points at the fact they think is invented. The phone reveals it ("invented by the
    game"), shows the four true facts with their sources, and keeps score (a point for each player who found
    it; a point for the expert for each player fooled).
  What would make it THE SAME GAME: an app, web game, board/card game or TV/radio format in which a player
  is handed (by the game, not written by themselves) a set of real facts that hides one false/invented
  fact, presents them all as true, and the others must find the false one; ideally with sources.

REPORT A - APP STORES (Apple App Store, Google Play, and app-review sites that came up)
Helper A, search done 5-6 Oct 2026, for the idea "Expert Interview" (Logicers)
=====================================================================================

SHORT ANSWER
  No SAME game found in the app stores within 12 searches.
  The stores are full of three kinds of apps:
    (1) personal "Two Truths and a Lie" apps (the player writes facts about themselves, mostly played remotely),
    (2) Fibbage/Balderdash clones (the app gives a true fact or question and everyone writes fake answers),
    (3) imposter / secret-word games (what "fake expert" and "bluff expert" searches mostly return).
  I found no app where the APP hands one player a set of real facts that hides one fake made up by the app,
  the player presents them, and the others find the fake. I also found no app that shows sources.
  The closest relatives turned up OUTSIDE the stores, in reviews that came up in my results:
    - "The Unbelievable Truth" board game (Ginger Fox) - CLOSE RELATIVE (the closest product)
    - a classroom version of "The Unbelievable Truth" (tutor2u) - CLOSE RELATIVE (closest in how the facts and lies are mixed)
    - "The Game of Lies" (2019 board game) and "Fake That!" (Studio H card game) - CLOSE RELATIVE
  Closest APPS: LiarLiar (Call My Bluff style, with an interrogator) - CLOSE RELATIVE; the Two Truths and a Lie
  apps - CLOSE RELATIVE; "Lie or Legend" (solo daily game: five statements a day, real or lie) - FAR as a party
  game, but very close in what the content looks like.

COMPARISON: HOW THE CLOSEST RELATIVES DIFFER FROM THE IDEA
==========================================================
Idea: the GAME writes 1 invented fact; 4 real facts with sources; about a country; ONE presenter who
alone knows which is invented; presents in own words + up to 3 questions; everyone votes for the
invented one; +1 to each finder, +1 to the presenter per player fooled; sources shown at the end.

1. Would I Lie to You? - Home Truths (BBC TV) - CLOSE RELATIVE
   Who writes the lie: producers ("programme researchers"). Who knows: the reader (about their own
   life). What is true: either the whole statement or nothing (binary). Unit: ONE personal statement.
   Scoring: team point for a right guess, point to the other team for a wrong guess. Sources: none.
2. The Unbelievable Truth (BBC Radio 4; Australian TV 2012; Ginger Fox board game) - CLOSE RELATIVE
   Who writes it: the panellist (Australian TV: "They write their own material", script-edited); the
   board game: the card. Who knows: the lecturer. What is true: 5 items (board game: 3 in bold), the
   rest is lies - THE REVERSE ratio. Scoring: buzz-in race, +1/-1 for challengers, lecturer +1 per
   unspotted truth. Sources: none seen. No questions.
3. tutor2u "Unbelievable Truth" classroom version - CLOSE RELATIVE
   Who writes the lies: the student (five lies, listed for the teacher). What is true: the rest of the
   speech (the SAME direction as the idea). Scoring: team buzz-ins; the speaker's team scores for lies
   not found. Sources: none.
4. Cambridge ESL "That's not right!" - CLOSE RELATIVE
   Who writes the lie: the students. What is true: 5 of 6 statements about a country. Others find the
   false one and correct it. Scoring: not shown. Sources: none.
5. "Two Truths, One Lie: History & Geography Challenge" (Payhip) - CLOSE RELATIVE
   Who writes the lie: the game (printed cards; categories include Countries). What is true: 2 of 3.
   Presenter/bluffer: not confirmed. Answers with "brief explanations for the lies"; no sources.
6. The Game of Lies (board game, 2019/2020) - CLOSE RELATIVE (two partial mechanics; see Query 12).
7. Call My Bluff (TV), Bluff the Listener (NPR), Liar's Club (TV) - CLOSE RELATIVES of the "bluff
   panel" family: several presenters, ONE item each, find the TRUE one; who writes the bluffs is not
   established from the pages I opened.
8. The Big Fib (Disney+ 2020) - CLOSE in theme (expert, questions, find the fibber); the fake is a
   person, not a fact.
Everything else (Fibbage, Talking Points, two truths and a lie apps, improv experts, quizzes): FAR.

What seems new in the idea, judging from my area only: the COMBINATION of (1) the game writes the
one invented fact and marks it only for the presenter, (2) it hides among REAL facts about one
topic (a country), (3) one presenter performs them in their own words with questions, and (4) the
reveal shows sources for the true facts. Each piece has a relative. I found no format that does all
four. Weak spot to know about: "find the false fact about a country" is already an ordinary ESL
classroom activity, so the new part is the combination, not the core guessing.

KNOWN RELATIVES IN MY AREA - EXACT DIFFERENCES (summary)
======================================================================
- The Unbelievable Truth (board game, Ginger Fox): the mirror image. Card = lecture with 3 hidden TRUTHS
  in nonsense; spot the truths by bell during the talk; lecturer scores per truth unnoticed. Idea = 4 truths
  + 1 hidden INVENTION; spoken in own words; up to 3 questions; one vote after; sources.
- Would I Lie to You? (board game, 2nd ed.): the card says truth or lie; when it is a lie the PLAYER
  invents it; one personal statement at a time; opposing team questions and guesses true/false.
- Call My Bluff (board game, Milton Bradley 1965): word definitions; the player makes up one of four;
  betting on True/Bluff.
- Two Truths and a Lie (card decks): Payhip printable (countries/history/people, 2+1, explanations for the
  lies) and Union Square & Co. literature deck (2+1, extra fun fact); play format not stated on the pages.
- Balderdash, Malarky, Sounds Fishy, Catfished, Fake That!, What The Actual Factual, Bamboozle (app,
  Fibbage-style), Fact or Fiction, True or Bluff: all hide the ONE TRUE answer among bluffs, and in all
  but True or Bluff / Fact or Fiction the bluffs are WRITTEN BY PLAYERS. The idea reverses both.
- Fibbage-style board games: only What The Actual Factual (Pikkii) and the Bamboozle app turned up.
- "Fake expert" party games: Mansplaining (expert monologue + secret words to spot, no true/false);
  "Expert Interviews" speaking game and improv "Word at a Time Expert" (no detection, no scoring).
- Not found in my 12 searches: a board game of Call My Bluff other than Milton Bradley's; "Fact or Crap";
  any Fibbage board game by Jackbox.
