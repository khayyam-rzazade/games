VERDICT (C): no SAME game was found, so it was built, as a mixture of known parts.
  The test for SAME (the brief below): a group plays TOGETHER as one team against the game, through a route of
  stops round the world (or a ladder of levels), with ONE challenge per stop answered by the player whose turn it
  is (turns rotate), about real countries, with a SHARED pool of lives and a LIMITED number of times the group may
  confer; ideally on one device passed round.
  Close relatives (told to Khayyam by a short message at about 19:35 on 6 Oct, with the offer to stop; he did not
  say stop before the build went on):
  - Tenable (ITV, 2016-2024): a team of five plays together against the game. In each round one player answers
    alone and "may not confer"; asking a teammate ("Nominate") is allowed at most three times in the whole game; in
    the final the players answer in a fixed order and a wrong answer puts that player out. No route, no lives
    shared as such, top-ten lists rather than one challenge per stop; a studio show.
  - QuizWiz (Brain Games, a card game): co-operative, about real countries, turns, and a limited number of
    "Debates" when the team may talk. No route, no lives; the answers are checked together at the end.
  - Bridge of Lies (BBC One, 2022 on): one team crosses a path of true and false statements, one player at a time;
    a "panic button" once per show; in the final a wrong step puts a player out and "the next player continues at
    the same point". General knowledge, not countries; the final allows free conferring.
  - Passaporte Mundo (Jogo na Mesa, Brazil, 2024; designer Rodrigo Rego): tagged on BoardGameGeek and on copies of
    its data as a co-operative trivia game, "an exciting adventure where cultural knowledge will serve as your
    ticket to the next challenge", 1 to 6 players. Its rules could not be found or read (two searches by Claude;
    its BoardGameGeek page refused the reader). It is the one title whose theme matches; worth a look by hand.
  Further: Noris "Reise ins Weltall" (a co-operative space quiz: one shared piece on a track, the card read to the
  player on the left, who answers alone or asks the team, help halves the move), Quiztopia (Kosmos, co-operative,
  free talk), WAS IST WAS "Entdecke die Welt" (Kosmos, co-operative, joker chips), Jackbox "Legends of Trivia"
  (2025, co-operative trivia against monsters, each on their own phone), Trivia Tricks (Steam, co-op bosses),
  The Crystal Maze, Codenames Duet's campaign map of world cities (co-operative, limited mistakes, no quiz),
  The Mind (levels, shared lives, no quiz), Kahoot's team mode ("team talk" before every question, team against
  team), 3D Atlas 98 (a circuit of the earth on one computer, a race), the classroom game "Around the World", and
  many solo "higher or lower" country apps. The three helpers' reports are below, word for word (part 3); every
  rating in them is the helper's own.
  What is this game's own: a route of ten real countries round the world, one question per stop with the real
  figures and the source at once, the turn-holder answering alone, two times to ask the table, three lives shared
  by the table, a wrong answer that keeps the table at the same stop, and one phone passed round.

THE IDEA TO CHECK (working name "The Table against the Game", for the site Logicers, logicers.com)
  A party game on ONE phone (or laptop) for 2 to 8 people at the same table. Co-operative: the whole table
  is ONE team, playing against the game.
  - The phone sets a journey: a route of about ten stops round the world, one real country per stop.
  - The phone is passed round. At each stop the player holding it gets ONE quick challenge about that
    country, made from real data (World Bank figures, checked lists), for example "Which has more people:
    Kenya or Canada?", "Which drives on the left: Japan or South Korea?", higher or lower, true or false.
  - The holder answers ALONE; the others may not help, except a few times per journey (for example twice)
    when the table calls a "huddle" and everyone may talk about that one challenge.
  - A right answer moves the table on to the next stop and the phone goes to the next player. A wrong
    answer costs one of the table's shared lives (for example three).
  - The journey ends at the last stop or when the lives are gone. Result: how far the table got and how
    many lives were left ("a perfect journey" if none was lost). The challenges get harder along the route.
    The real figures and their sources are shown after each answer.

  What would make it THE SAME GAME: an app, web game, board/card game or TV/radio format in which a group
  plays TOGETHER as one team against the game (co-operative), going through a route of stops round the
  world (or a ladder of levels), with ONE challenge per stop answered by the player whose turn it is (turns
  rotate), about real countries, with a SHARED pool of lives, and a LIMITED number of times the group may
  confer. Ideally on one device passed round. Say exactly which of these parts each find has and lacks.

KNOWN RELATIVES (look at them closely where they fall in your area and say exactly how they differ):
  TV: The Chase (a team against the Chaser; the Final Chase is played together), Eggheads, Tenable (a team
  of five plays together), The Cube (lives, "simplify", "trial run"), Who Wants to Be a Millionaire (a
  ladder of questions and lifelines), The Wall, Bridge of Lies, The Crystal Maze (a team; lock-ins and
  buy-outs), Fort Boyard, Hive Minds, Quizduell (the studio against the country), the Carmen Sandiego TV
  game show. Games: Pandemic (co-operative, round the world), Hanabi, The Mind and ito (co-operative, shared
  lives or levels), Codenames Duet (co-operative, a campaign map of cities with limited mistakes), the
  Carmen Sandiego board and card games, Kahoot "team mode" ("team talk"), Netflix "Trivia Quest" and

7. PRECEDENTS FOR SINGLE PARTS OF THE IDEA (from the pages above)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Limited help or huddles: Tenable "Nominate" (at most 3 per game) and
  "Overrule" (once per round); Bridge of Lies panic button (once per show);
  Hive Minds "two passes"; The Cube "Simplify" (once).
- Players as the team's lives, and play continues where it stopped: the
  Bridge of Lies final ("the next player continues at the same point");
  the Tenable final (a wrong answer eliminates that player); Eggheads
  (losers miss the final).
- Answering alone with no conferring: Tenable ("may not confer with anyone
  else"); Bridge of Lies solo crossings.
- Turns that rotate inside one team: Tenable final (fixed order); Hive
  Minds ("Play then passes to the next player"); Family Fortunes (answers
  "down the line").
- A route of stops: Crystal Maze zones; Bridge of Lies path; 3D Atlas 98
  "complete circuit of the earth"; the library game's passport of six
  countries; the classroom "Around the World".
- One device passed between teams: 9Guess ("Pass-and-Play", but teams
  compete); 3D Atlas 98 (turns on one computer, competitive).

PART 1. BOTTOM LINE
======================================================================================================
- No SAME game found in board and card games.
- Closest finds, each a CLOSE RELATIVE:
  1. QUIZWIZ (Brain Games Publishing): co-operative, about REAL COUNTRIES, turns, and a LIMITED number of
     "Debates" when the team may talk (a direct parallel to the huddle). Lacks the route, one-answer-per-turn
     with instant result, and shared lives (answers are all checked at the end).
  2. NORIS "REISE INS WELTALL" (space quiz, Art.Nr. 60 601 1985): the turn loop is almost the idea's loop:
     co-operative; one shared piece on a track; the card is read to the player on the left, who answers alone
     or "asks the team for help" (help costs half the move); a fixed card budget (15/12/9 by difficulty);
     the table loses when the cards run out. Lacks countries, counted lives, and a hard limit on help.
  3. QUIZTOPIA (Kosmos, Marc-Uwe Kling): co-operative quiz against the game with a 24-card clock, but
     discussion is free and even encouraged, the opposite of "answer alone".
  4. WAS IST WAS "ENTDECKE DIE WELT" (Kosmos, children 8-12): co-operative, a shared piece wanders on,
     joker chips help with hard questions. General knowledge, not countries.
- One lead I could not check: PASSAPORTE MUNDO (Rodrigo Rego, 2024, BGG 413920), tagged co-operative trivia,
  "adventure around the world", 1-6 players. Only a machine-written blurb seen; its BGG page was blocked.
  It is the one title whose theme matches; worth checking by hand.
- Structure relatives (no quiz): Codenames Duet (a campaign over a world map of cities, each city with its
  own turns and allowed mistakes) and The Mind (levels, shared lives, throwing stars) carry the
  "journey/levels + limited mistakes" skeleton.
- Geography and travel quiz games found (Carmen Sandiego board and card games, Noris "Weltreise fuer Kinder",
  Noris "Rund um die Welt", The World Game, Let's Jet, Professor Noggin, Das Laender-Quiz, Twinkl's travel
  game): all competitive (first or most wins). 10 Days in Europe: competitive, no quiz.
- Names: no board/card game found called exactly Same Boat, Beat the Phone, Ten Stops or Round Trip.
  Near: "In the Same Boat" (co-operative sailing VIDEO game, Steam); "STOPS: North American Edition"
  (geography trivia); "Round Trip: Taiwan" (2025 travel card game) and "Planes: Round Trip" (AEG expansion).

A2. NORIS SPACE QUIZ, file name "reiseinsweltall" (Reise ins Weltall), Art.Nr. 60 601 1985  RATING: CLOSE RELATIVE
    https://cdn.simba-dickie-group.de/downloads/606011985/606011985_reiseinsweltall_noris.pdf
      "Dieses Spiel ist kooperativ, d.h. ihr spielt gemeinsam." (This game is co-operative, you play together.)

      "Wenn du dir nicht sicher bist, kannst du dein Team um Hilfe bitten." (If you are not sure, you can ask

    https://opinionatedgamers.com/2017/09/04/codenames-duet-game-review-by-chris-wray/

    https://casualgamerevolution.com/node/1799
      "This shows a map of the world and players can play across the different cities."
      "Each city has its own mission parameters which lists the number of turns players have (how many

    https://rulespal.com/codenames-duet/rulebook
      "Use 9 timer tokens"; "If either player guesses a word that the other player sees as black, your team

      voluntarily or by guessing a bystander, they become the clue-giver for the next turn."; "The free
      Codenames Companion includes a mission map for Duet".
    DIFFERS: word-association clues, not quiz questions or real data; made for two (or two halves of a
    group); turns and mistakes are set afresh in each city, not one pool for the whole journey; talk is
    limited to clues all the time, rather than banned with a few huddles. SHARES: a journey across world
    cities, a challenge per city, a cap on mistakes, alternating roles.

B2. THE MIND (Wolfgang Warsch, 2018)     RATING: CLOSE RELATIVE in structure, FAR in content
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mind_(card_game)
      "Players attempt to play hands of numbered cards in correct ascending order without communicating."
      "played over an amount of levels depending on the number of players (eight rounds in a four-player
      game, ten rounds in a three-player game, and twelve rounds in a two-player game)"
      "Players have throwing stars which they can use during a round if a silent vote to use one is
      unanimous, causing each player to simultaneously discard the lowest numbered card from their hand
      before play continues."
      "Players win if they complete all required levels, and lose if they run out of lives at any point."
    https://gamerules.com/rules/the-mind/
      "the team is given a specific number of Life cards" (two, three, four by player count); "The group then

      openly nor secretly." (This page says play goes "until level twelve has been reached" for any player
      count, which conflicts with Wikipedia.)
    DIFFERS: no questions; everyone plays at once in real time (no turns); no talking ever; throwing stars
    are group-agreed special moves (a loose parallel to huddles). SHARES: levels getting harder, a shared
    pool of lives, a few special group moves.
