BEAT THE PHONE: how the game is made (6 Oct 2026, evening)
=========================================================

What it is
  A game for one phone and two to eight players, the fourth game for groups ("Pass the phone" on the home page).
  Working name "The Table against the Game" (group-games.md 1.6: the co-op ideas of the long list, group G).
  The whole table is one team, playing against the phone. The phone sets a journey of ten stops round the world, one
  country per stop. At each stop the player holding the phone gets one question about that country: two countries,
  pick the one that fits ("Which has more people?"). They answer alone. Right: the table moves on to the next stop.
  Wrong: the table loses one of its three lives, and the next player tries again at the same stop, with a new
  question. Twice a journey the one holding the phone may ask the table: then everyone may talk about that question.
  Reach the tenth stop and the table beats the phone; lose the third life and the journey ends where the table is.
  The questions get harder along the route. Not daily: no day number, streak or album.
  Everything below marked (C) in the plan was decided by Claude by Khayyam's five rules.

The start, in ten seconds
  "One team, ten stops, three lives." and "The whole table plays against the phone. Pass it round: whoever holds it
  answers alone. Right, and you move on to the next stop; wrong, and you lose a life. Twice a journey, you may ask the
  table." Players 2 to 8 with - and + (3 at first), "Names" (optional: a window with one field per player), Start, and
  "2 to 8 players · one phone · about 10 minutes · no map".

A turn
  1. Passing the phone (as in Still In and So-Called Expert; Khayyam, 5 Oct 2026, late evening): a whole screen in
     olive says what just happened ("A new journey: ten stops round the world, and three lives.", "Right! On to stop
     5 of 10.", or "Wrong. One life lost: 2 lives left. A new question at the same stop."), "Pass the phone to" and the
     player's name in big letters, "Stop 5 of 10: Kenya. Bo answers alone.", and the button "Bo has the phone", which
     that player taps. Under it: the lives left, how often the table may still be asked, and "The route" (a window
     with the ten stops and every question so far). A reload while it shows brings it back.
  2. The stop: on top the route as a line of ten dots (cleared ones full, the current one ringed, a small yellow dot
     under a stop where a life was lost; a tap opens the journey) and the three lives as hearts (a lost one yellow);
     "Stop 4 of 10 · Eastern Africa" (the UN's sub-region), the country in big letters, then the question card in olive:
     "Bo answers alone", the question ("Which has more people?") and what it counts ("How many people live there").
     The two countries as two big answers, in alphabetical order; one tap picks, the big button says "Go with Kenya"
     and answers (one slipped thumb never costs a life). Under the answers: "Stuck? Ask the table" with "2 left".
  3. Asking the table: the button goes, and the line says "Everyone may talk about this one. Bo gives the answer."
     The question card says "Bo asks the table". The journey keeps who asked.
  4. The answer: both answers turn over and show their figures ("✓ 56.4 million" on the right one, "✗" and yellow on a
     wrong pick), and the line says "Right!" or "Wrong." with one sentence per country ("Kenya has 56.4 million
     people. Canada has 41.3 million people.") and, when the journey ends, "That was the tenth stop!" or "That was the
     last life."; under it the source ("Source: World Bank: Population, total (2025; CC BY 4.0)", or for a list
     "Checked on Wikipedia · WorldAtlas"). The big button: "Pass the phone" (the next player in order), or "See how it
     ended".
  5. The end: "Journey over", "You beat the phone!" ("Round the world in ten stops, with 2 lives left." or "Round the
     world in ten stops, without losing a life. A perfect journey!") or "The phone wins." ("Your journey ended in
     Kenya, at stop 7 of 10."); the ten stops in two columns (✓ for cleared, a yellow dot for each life lost there, the
     stop where it ended in yellow, the stops not reached faded); "Your table's best: ..." (or "A new best for your
     table: ...!"); Play again (the same players; the next journey starts with the next player), Share, "Every question"
     (the window with every question, who answered, the right answer, both figures and the source), "New players".

The route: ten stops round the world (C)
  - The stops are UN members with at least a million people (156 on the figures of 5 Oct 2026), as in Still In:
    figures of very small countries are often odd (the World Bank's area of Monaco), and they are hard to compare.
  - The way round the world: the UN's sub-regions in one eastward loop (CYCLE in beat-the-phone.js): Northern,
    Western, Southern and Eastern Europe; Northern, Western, Middle, Southern and Eastern Africa; Western, Central,
    Southern, South-eastern and Eastern Asia; Melanesia, Australia and New Zealand; South, Central America, the
    Caribbean, Northern America; and back to Europe.
  - A journey: two stops in each region, one in Oceania, and one more in Europe, Africa, Asia or the Americas (at
    random); within a region different sub-regions; one country in each, preferring countries the table has not had
    in its last 40 stops. Oceania's stop is one of its three countries of a million people (Australia, New Zealand,
    Papua New Guinea). The journey starts at a sub-region at random and keeps to the loop's order.
  - No map (Khayyam's brief): the route is a line of dots and a list of names.

The questions (C)
  - Always two countries, one of them the stop: "which of the two fits". 17 kinds:
    nine World Bank figures, the one with more of it: "Which has more people?" (people), "Which is bigger?" (total
    area in km²), "Where do people live longer?" (life expectancy at birth), "Which is more urban?" (the share in
    towns and cities), "Which has more forest?" (the share of the land), "Which is richer per person?" (GDP per
    person, adjusted for prices), "Which is more online?" (the share using the internet), "Which is more crowded?"
    (people per km² of land), "Which is younger?" (the share under 15). Never the one with less (no question ranks
    countries by what they lack, as in Still In).
    eight checked lists of One of 193, the one on the list: "Which is landlocked?", "Which is an island country?",
    "Which has a coast on the Mediterranean?", "Which does the equator cross?", "Which borders China?", "Which borders
    Russia?", "Which drives on the left?", "Which uses the euro?".
  - Each question says what it counts, under the question and in the sentences after the answer.
  - The other country of a figure question is one of Still In's 45 well-known bar countries from every continent
    (an anchor people know: "Malawi or Australia?"); of a list question, a country from the same UN sub-region
    (else the same region) that is on the other side of the list (Portugal next to Spain for the Mediterranean,
    Ethiopia next to Kenya for the equator, Switzerland next to Germany for the euro). Never the country the list
    is about ("borders China" never asks about China); Australia never in "an island country" (a continent or an
    island: sources and people differ); the United Kingdom never in "a coast on the Mediterranean" (a country counts
    with its own land only, as in One of 193, but its overseas territory Gibraltar has a Mediterranean coast: a
    player who knows that should not lose a life for it). The help says: "a country counts with its own land only,
    without overseas territories".
  - The stop is the answer about half the time (49.8% over 90,000 questions of 3,000 journeys), so that a table
    cannot learn "it is always the other one".
  - Fair, as in Still In: two figures never lie within the margin (people 5%, area 3%, GDP per person 8%, people per
    km² 5%, life expectancy 1 year, towns and cities 3 points, forest 2 points, online 3 points, under 15 1.5
    points), so that a small revision cannot change an answer, and never look the same once rounded for the screen.
  - Harder along the route (the levels): stops 1 to 3 big differences (more than 4 times as many people, more than 7
    years, more than 20 points: Still In's "far"), stops 4 to 6 middle ones (1.6 to 4 times, 2.5 to 7 years, 8 to 20
    points), stops 7 and 8 middle or close, stops 9 and 10 close calls (within 1.6 times, 2.5 years, 8 points; 1.5
    times for GDP; 4 points for under 15). A list question comes from the third stop on. Where a stop has no fair
    question at its level, an easier level is taken (never a harder one).
  - Three questions per stop are made when the journey starts: the first try, and the new questions for the next
    players after a wrong answer (three lives allow at most three tries at one stop). The first tries of two stops in
    a row never ask the same thing; a stop's tries never repeat a kind; no kind more than twice among the ten first
    tries; at most three lists among them, never two in a row; the comparison countries change as long as there are
    new ones.
  - A journey is made from its seed, so that a friend's link deals the same ten stops and, while the figures stay
    the same, the same questions.
  - How often a table goes round the world, by a guess of how often people are right (big differences 92%, middle
    78%, close 64%, lists 76%, asking the table +12 points, used at the last stops): an average table about 56% of
    journeys, a perfect journey 13%; a strong table about 82%, a weak one 28%. A guess until the friends play it.

Where its facts come from
  - data/one-of-193.js (made by r/make-one-of-193.R): the 193 UN member states, their UN regions and sub-regions,
    the eight lists checked on two websites each (their quotes are in r/one-of-193-workshop/evidence.txt), and five
    World Bank figures: people, surface area, life expectancy, the share in towns and cities, forest.
  - data/still-in.js (made by r/make-still-in.R): four more World Bank figures: GDP per person (PPP), people
    online, people per km² of land, people under 15.
  - Every figure is the World Bank's open data under CC BY 4.0 (the scripts check the licence of each), each
    country's latest figure at most six years old; its year stands under every answer.
  - No new figure and no new list were needed, so there is no R script and no evidence file of its own.
  - Without data/still-in.js the game plays with the five figures of One of 193 and the lists.

What the browser keeps (under "groups" > "beat-the-phone" in the site's one store; never under the daily games)
  n (players), names (eight, optional, cleaned, at most 14 letters), best ({s: stops cleared, l: lives left}; more
  stops is better, then more lives), journeys, world (journeys round the world), perfect, next (who starts the next
  journey), recent (the last 40 stops), cur (a journey in progress: the players, the route, the seed, the questions
  of every stop, the stops cleared, the lives, the times left to ask the table, whose turn, who started, every
  answer [stop, try, player, country picked, right, asked the table], and whether the phone is being passed, a
  question is open or its answer is shown). A journey whose story does not add up (or whose questions are no longer
  fair on today's data) is left aside. Nothing counts in the Today card, the streak, the way onward or the
  returning-player events. The privacy line ("table" in turnsout.js): "The players' names and your table's best
  journey are kept only in this browser."

Sharing (C)
  The picture (1080 by 1350, olive): "Logicers" and "Beat the Phone", "Ten stops round the world, three lives:", "We
  beat the phone!" or "We got to stop 7.", "Round the world with 2 lives left." (or "without losing a life", or "The
  phone won at stop 7 of 10."), the three lives as hearts, the ten stops in two columns with a line through them
  (cleared ones white, the stop where it ended yellow, the others faded), "Can your table beat the phone?" and "Play
  at logicers.com". Never a question or an answer. The message: "Beat the Phone: our table went round the world in
  ten stops with 2 lives left. Can your table beat the phone? <link>" (or "got to stop 7 of 10 before the phone won").
  The link (?j=ten codes&s=seed&k=stops cleared&l=lives left) deals the same journey to a friend's table: "A friend's
  table played this route of ten stops and got to stop 7 of 10. Can your table beat the phone?", "Play their route".
  A link whose questions are no longer fair on today's data is left aside.

Counter events (GoatCounter): beat-the-phone/started, /finished, /share, /challenge-opened; none names a country or
  a player.

Its colour: olive, #6F6C00 by day (tint #EEEFD8), #BFD957 at night (tint #161906). In OKLab 0.098 from the nearest
  game colour by day (green, Missing Piece) and 0.095 at night (amber, Half); the closest pair of earlier games
  (teal and green) lie 0.059 apart. White text on it: 5.5 to 1. (search.txt has the search for it.)

The checks: r/site-workshop/checks/t_beat.py (see its head).
To run again after a new run of r/make-one-of-193.R or r/make-still-in.R: nothing to make; run t_beat.py (it reads
  both data files) before the push.
