STILL IN: how the game is made (5 Oct 2026, evening)
=====================================================

What it is
  A game for one phone and two to eight players, the second game for groups ("Pass the phone" on the home page).
  Twelve countries and one rule ("More people than Spain"). The players take turns tapping a country that fits;
  the phone checks it against the World Bank's figures (or a checked list) and shows the real figure. A wrong
  tap puts that player out of the game. When no country that fits is left, a new rule comes, a little harder.
  The last player still in wins. Not daily: no day number, streak or album.

Where its facts come from
  - data/one-of-193.js (made by r/make-one-of-193.R): the 193 UN member states, their UN regions, the eight lists
    checked on two websites each (their quotes are in r/one-of-193-workshop/evidence.txt), and five World Bank
    figures: people, surface area, life expectancy, the share in towns and cities, forest (share of land).
  - data/still-in.js (made by r/make-still-in.R, which Khayyam runs in RStudio): four more World Bank figures that
    One of 193 does not have: GDP per person (PPP, current international $), people online (share), people per
    square kilometre of land, people under 15 (share). The table of them is figures.csv here.
  - Every figure is the World Bank's open data under CC BY 4.0 (the scripts check the licence of each), each
    country's latest figure at most six years old; the year stands with every figure in the game.
  - No new list was made for Still In, so there is no evidence.txt here: the lists are One of 193's.
  - Without data/still-in.js the game plays with the five figures of One of 193 and the lists.

The rules (22; the words are in still-in/still-in.js, RULES)
  Figures, against a bar country (dir +1: more than the bar, -1: less):
    more-people, fewer-people (pop); bigger, smaller (area); longer-lives (life); more-urban, less-urban (urban);
    more-forest, less-forest (forest); richer (gdp); more-online (net); more-crowded, less-crowded (dense);
    younger (young: a bigger share under 15).
  Never asked (C): shorter lives, poorer per person, fewer online, older: they would rank countries by what they lack.
  Lists: landlocked, island, med (a coast on the Mediterranean), equator, china, russia (a land border), left
    (traffic keeps to the left), euro.
  Left out (C): regions (where Cyprus or Georgia stands is a convention, and a wrong tap puts a player out),
    income groups, coasts on the oceans (the sources do not agree), anything about religion, politics or people.

Who comes into a pool
  The UN members with at least a million people (156 on the figures of 5 Oct 2026). A country without the rule's
  figure never comes into that rule's pools (for example North Korea for GDP per person).

The bars (BAR_IDS): 45 well-known countries from every continent
  Spain, France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, Poland, Sweden, Norway, Greece, Portugal, the Netherlands,
  Switzerland, Ireland, Finland, Austria; the United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Peru,
  Colombia; China, India, Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines, Pakistan, Bangladesh,
  Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia; Egypt, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Ethiopia, Morocco, Ghana; Australia,
  New Zealand. A rule takes a bar at random among those that give a fair pool, and not one used lately.

Fair pools (checked by r/site-workshop/checks/t_still.py, G1 to G5)
  - Twelve different countries, none of them the bar, in alphabetical order on the screen.
  - Five, six or seven of them fit (at random, so that nobody can count on it). The phone never says how many.
  - The margin: no country lies this close to the bar, so that a small revision of a figure cannot change an answer:
    people 5%, area 3%, GDP per person 8%, people per km² 5% (as ratios); life expectancy 1 year; towns and cities
    3 points, forest 2 points, online 3 points, under 15 1.5 points. And no country looks the same as the bar on
    screen once both are rounded (the tiles show whole percentages, millions with one decimal and so on).
  - Close calls: a "near" country lies within 1.6 times the bar (1.5 for GDP; 2.5 years; 8 points; 4 points for
    under 15). Each side of the bar gets near, middle and far countries: the first rule of a game a third of each
    (at least one near on each side), the second half near, from the third on two thirds near (at least two near on
    each side). So the obvious ones go first and every rule is harder than the one before.
  - A list rule: five to seven countries on the list, and the others from the same UN sub-regions first, then the
    same regions, then anywhere (Equatorial Guinea next to Gabon for the equator, Portugal next to Spain for the
    Mediterranean).
  - The order of rules in a game: it opens with a figure and its bar; a rule, a figure or a list never comes twice
    in one game (until all have come); two list rules never follow each other.

A turn
  Whoever holds the phone taps a country (it is picked: the button says "Check Vietnam") and checks it. The tile
  turns over and shows the figure (✓ in the game's colour, or ✗ in yellow). When the turn passes to another player,
  a whole screen comes up instead (since 5 Oct 2026, late evening: Khayyam found the line under the grid too easy to
  miss): what just happened ("Right. Vietnam has 101.6 million people." or "Out! ... Ben is out."), "Pass the phone
  to" and the next player's name in big letters, and the button "Ana has the phone", which the next player taps.
  The same screen comes at the start of a game ("A new game: twelve countries, one rule.") and before every new rule
  ("Rule 2 is next, a little harder."). On the board
  the line under the grid then says "Ana's turn. Tap a country that fits." The whole figure in words keeps its year
  where it is not the bar's year. In a sentence the
  names that need it take "the" (the Netherlands, the United States, the Central African Republic: 14 names, the
  list THE in still-in.js); the tiles and the list after a rule show the bare name. After the last
  country that fits: "That was the last one that fits", the button "See all twelve", then the twelve in order
  (from the biggest figure down, the bar where it falls; for a list, the countries on it first), who tapped which,
  and the sources; "Next rule". After the last player but one went out: the button "Who is still in?", then the end:
  the winner, who went out on what (in the order they went out), the wins at this table, Play again, Share,
  "The last twelve", "New players".

What the browser keeps (under "groups" > "still-in" in the site's one store; never under the daily games)
  games (how many finished) and recent (the last 40 rules with their bars). Broken data is left aside.
  Since 6 Oct 2026 (Khayyam: the names got stuck; the game must restart when you go to the home page and come back,
  the names every time the game is opened, and a Restart button) nothing about the players is stored: the number of
  players and their names (eight, optional, cleaned, at most 14 letters), the wins at this table ({name: count}),
  who starts the next game and the game going on live only while the page is open. Every opening of the page (from
  the home page, the Back button, a reload, or a page the browser brings back from its memory) starts afresh with
  3 players and no names; what earlier versions stored about the players (n, names, wins, next, cur) is deleted
  when the page opens. The round button ↻ in the bar (only while a game is in play) asks "Restart the game?" and
  then starts everything over the same way; so does "New players" at the end. "Play again" keeps the players and
  their wins. A pull on the page does not reload it (overscroll-behavior), so that a game is not lost by accident.

Sharing
  The picture (1080 by 1350, plum) shows the rule of the game that put the most players out, the bar's figure or the
  list's meaning, its twelve countries without a mark, how many players it put out, and "Can your table get through
  it?". The link (?r=rule&b=bar&c=twelve codes&k=players out) deals the same twelve and rule to a friend's table as
  its first rule; a link that is no longer fair on today's figures is left aside.

Counter events (GoatCounter): still-in/started, still-in/finished, still-in/share, still-in/challenge-opened.

Its colour: plum, #6E2C78 by day (tint #F4E5F7), #E27FF5 at night (tint #1E1024). In OKLab 0.142 from the nearest
  game colour by day (violet) and 0.088 at night (violet); the closest pair of earlier games (teal and green) lie
  0.059 apart. White text on it: 9.15 to 1.

To run the data maker again: open r/make-still-in.R in RStudio and press Source (about once a year, when the World
  Bank adds a year). Then a chat runs t_still.py on the new file before the push.
To test the R script without the internet: python3 test/pretend_wb.py <folder> [full|gaps|licence|renamed|unknown],
  then Rscript test/test_r.R <folder> <copy of the site> [nonet].
