VERDICT (C): the same game was not found; built as a mixture.
  Not found anywhere: one phone passed round a group; a pool of real countries shown to all; a rule against a bar
  country (or a checked list); each pick checked against open data with the real figure shown; a wrong pick puts the
  player out of the whole game; a new rule when no country that fits is left; the last player in wins.

  The closest relative is the board game SMART10 (2017; Arno Steinwender and Christoph Reiser; Martinex, Piatnik,
  Bananagrams and others). On its "true/false" cards ten items are shown to all, players take turns picking one they
  think is true ("You can only go for the correct 'true' answers", rulespal.com), the pick is checked by lifting its
  marker, and a wrong pick puts that player out of the card. That is the same core turn. What differs: in Smart10 you
  may pass and keep your points; a wrong answer costs only that card; it is a points race to 30; true/false is one of
  six kinds of question, general trivia, printed on cards; there is no bar, no figure shown, no list in order after a
  card. Still In has no pass, puts a player out of the whole game, compares with a bar country, shows the real figure
  at every tap and every country in order after every rule, and deals its rounds from open data.

  Other relatives: Wipeout (US TV quiz, 1988, and its UK version: a board of answers with wrong ones hidden in it,
  picked in turn; a wrong pick costs money, not your place); JetPunk's one-player "Sudden Death" quizzes ("click every
  country in the list that has over 100 million population ... any wrong click ends the quiz"); the Tenable final
  (wrong answer, out of the game; answers from memory); the youth-group games "Last One Standing" (Download Youth
  Ministry: name things in turn, a wrong answer puts you out); Cardline: Globetrotter and GeoXpert (country cards placed
  by hidden area or population); the app Last One Standing - Celeb Quiz (one player, daily: tap the wrong celebrities).

CONCLUSIONS
======================================================================

(1) VERDICT: does the same game exist in the app stores?
In these 14 searches I found NO app with the full idea: one phone passed around, a
shared pool of countries on screen, a fact rule checked against real data, one pick
per turn, a wrong pick knocks you out, and the last player in wins.
Closest relatives, most similar first:
  a) Last One Standing - Celeb Quiz (tnySocial, App Store, Trivia). It has a shared
     pool of nine on screen, a factual question ("Who's the tallest?"), tapping one at
     a time, a shrinking pool, and three lives. But it is solo and daily, uses
     celebrities, and you remove the wrong ones. It also has the same name.
  b) Smart10 (board game, also a TV format on ORF 1). Players take turns giving one
     answer from a shared card of 10, and a wrong answer puts you out for that card.
     But it is points-based, you can pass, and it is general trivia. No official store
     app found; one page says it is on Tabletopia.
  c) 9Guess (App Store). Pass-and-play, 9 answers per question, with trap/bomb
     answers. But it uses teams, points and a timer, with no knock-out.
  d) TapRush (App Store). Pass-and-play with a shared pool of letters that shrinks.
     But players say words, the group judges them, and the timer decides who loses.
  e) BOMB: Party Word Game (Google Play). Pass-the-phone, and the holder is out when it
     explodes. But the group judges answers and there is no pool.
  f) Country-statistics apps: Higher or Lower: Population / Country Area, The Higher
     Lower Game, The Higher or Lower Game, More or Less, World Quiz, Quiz World
     Countries & Cities, GeoHunter, GeoRanks, GeoG. They share the same data theme
     but are mostly single-player two-country comparisons or rankings. GeoG has local
     pass-and-play. None has a shared pool with knock-outs.
  g) TapOut Trivia (App Store). A wrong answer means elimination, but it is live online
     multiple choice, not one phone.
Limits: 14 searches only. Web search covers app stores only partly, and several store
pages returned 404. "Not found" here does not prove that no such app exists.

(2) THE NAMES
- Last One Standing: USED, closest conflict. "Last One Standing - Celeb Quiz" is a
  trivia app (tnySocial, App Store) with a similar pick-and-shrink mechanic. Also used
  by "The Last One Standing" (football pick-a-team competition manager, App Store) and
  by a Google Play app "Last One Standing" (timuzgames; page 404, genre unknown). In
  football pools, "last one standing" is also a common name for the knock-out format.
- Still In: no app found (one combined search).
- Sure Thing: no app found (one combined search).
- Safe Pick: no game found. Only "Safe Pick Up" / "Safe Pickup" school pickup apps
  (a near spelling, different field).
- Thin Ice: used in game titles, but not by a quiz: "Danse Macabre: Thin Ice"
  (hidden-object adventure) and "Thin Ice Run Arena" (App Store result; page 404,
  genre unknown).
- Close Call: used. "Close Call" (casual 1v1 block-stacking game, App Store) and
  "Close Call" (business call tracker, Google Play). Two more Google Play results
  ("CLOSE CALL", "Close Call Live") returned 404. No quiz app found.
Caution: Still In, Sure Thing and Safe Pick were each covered by only one combined
search, so a quick check before choosing one would be worthwhile.

(1) VERDICT: does the same game exist on the web?
No exact match found. Within 14 searches I found no web game, app page, TV format or classroom game with the full design:
a pool of countries shown to all, 2 to 8 players taking turns on one device, each pick checked against real data with the figure
shown, a wrong pick knocking that player out, the pool getting harder, a new rule after each pool, last player in wins.
The idea is built from parts that each exist:
  a) The pick move, solo: JetPunk "Sudden Death" quizzes ("click every country in the list that has over 100 million population
     ... any wrong click ends the quiz!") and "Population battle" (rows get harder, 3 lives). Sporcle "minefield" quizzes share the
     "wrong click ends it" rule. These are the closest web relatives, but all are one-player.
  b) A shared board with hidden wrong answers, picked in turn: Wipeout (US 1988: 16 answers, 5 "wipeouts"; UK: 12 answers, 4 wrong).
     A wrong pick costs money or control, not your place. The Pointless round 2 board variant is similar.
  c) Turns + shrinking list + wrong answer = out: the Tenable final ("Wrong answer? Out of the game at once"), and group games
     "Last One Standing" (Download Youth Ministry) and "The Category Game" (last person standing wins). These are recall-based:
     no pool on screen, nothing checked against data.
  d) Countries judged against real rankings with the real list revealed: the Gamely card game "Top of the World" (not turns,
     not knock-out).
  The new part of the Logicers idea is the combination: a visible pool (like Wipeout/JetPunk) + turns and knock-out (like Tenable
  and "Last One Standing") + real country data with the figure and the sorted list shown after each pick and each pool.
  Limits: 14 searches only; the Listdle and Alldle directories could not be reached (search 6 failed), so they are unchecked;
  app stores and board-game databases were not searched as such. "Not found" is not proof that nothing exists.

- Setup: "See that the cards are placed correctly in the Smartbox - that is, the answers can be seen in the holes (B).
  Place the 10 answer markers on the provided holes in the Smartbox." (rulespal)
  So: one round card shows a question in the middle and ten items around it; a marker (peg) covers the answer to each item.

How a turn goes:
- "The youngest player starts. He reads out the question in the center of the card and gives the first answer.
  When it's your turn you can either answer or pass." (rulespal)
- Answer: "If you choose to answer, give an answer which you think is correct, by saying the answer out loud and
  remove the respective answer marker." (rulespal)
- Check: "After the player has chosen an answer, she/he raises the marker and checks whether it is correct." (Piatnik page)
- Right: "The answer was correct? Awesome! Keep the answer marker in front of you." (rulespal)
  Then the box goes to the next player, who (Piatnik page, as extracted) faces "nine possible answers" - the pool shrinks.

The "true or false" question type (the closest part to the idea):
- "True/false (white ring): The answers are either 'true' or 'false'. You can only go for the correct 'true' answers." (rulespal)
- "Some of them are true/false style, where you only want to pull the answer markers for the true answers." (PuzzleNation, https://puzzculture.com/tag/true/)
- Piatnik page: the game has "classic questions about general knowledge" and "special rounds with questions that require
  a true / false answer."
- So on these cards: ten items are shown to all; some fit the statement and some do not; on your turn you pick ONE item
  you think fits; a wrong pick knocks you out of that card.
- No example true/false question was printed on the pages I opened.
Other question types (rulespal): Number, Order, Century/Decade, Color, Open. (The Board Game Family lists True/False,
Number, Century/Decade, Order and Open.)

May you pass? YES.
- "Now you don't risk losing the markers you have already collected on this round! But you can't give any further
  answers for this card." (rulespal)
- "If they choose to pass, they're also out of the rest of the round." (Board Game Family)
- "If it's a difficult question it is better to pass, because if you give a wrong answer you will lose all markers of
  the current round." (Piatnik page)

What happens on a wrong answer: you are out of THAT CARD (round) only, not the game, and you lose that card's markers.
- "The answer was wrong? You drop out and can't give any more answers on this card." and "you lose all answer markers
  you gained from this card so far. (The points you have gathered in earlier rounds will remain.)" (rulespal)
- "If they're wrong, they discard the peg and are out of the round." (Board Game Family)

How points work:
- "All players now add their points to their scoring wheels and place the markers back on the Smartbox." (rulespal)
- "Once the round is over, you add a point for each answer marker to the total indicated by your scoring wheel on the
  Smartbox." (PuzzleNation)
- "Once the round ends, players who didn't answer incorrectly at all, count their pegs and tally their score." (Board Game Family)

How a round ends:
- "A round ends, when all 10 answer markers are removed, or when all players passed or dropped out." (rulespal)

How the game ends:
- "The game ends as soon as a player/team reaches at least 30 points in the scoring wheel. The player or team who then
  has the most points, is the winner." (rulespal)

It gets harder as the easy answers go (same feeling as the idea):
- "Choosing the 'easier' answers first will preclude others from taking those when it's their turn, giving you a chance
  to get more points when it comes back to you." (Board Game Family)

Caution: boardgamematcher's one-line summary says "everyone answers simultaneously", which does not match the rulebook
(players answer in turn, starting with the youngest). The rulebook is the better source.

SAME as the idea (on Smart10's true/false cards):
- one shared set of items shown to everyone (10 on the card; the idea has 12 countries);
- a factual rule, some items fit and some do not;
- players take turns; the holder picks ONE item they think fits;
- the pick is checked at once (lift the marker; in the idea the phone shows the real figure);
- right: the item leaves the pool and the box/phone passes on; wrong: that player is out;
- the pool shrinks, the easy picks go first, it gets harder;
- 2-8 players (boardgamematcher), the same range as the idea.
DIFFERENT from the idea:
- Smart10 lets you PASS (stop safely and keep your markers); the idea has no pass;
- a wrong pick in Smart10 only knocks you out of that card and costs that card's markers; in the idea it knocks you
  out of the whole game;
- Smart10 is a points race (first to 30); the idea is "last player still in wins";
- true/false is one of six question types; Smart10 is general trivia, not built on countries and statistics;
- no "bar" item to compare against, no real figures shown, no sorted reveal of all items with a source after each pool;
- a physical box with printed cards, versus one phone with live data (World Bank, checked lists).

VERDICT
======================================================================
(1) Does the same game exist as a board or card game?
Not exactly. I found no board or card game where players pick countries against a statistic "bar", a wrong pick
puts you out of the whole game, the last player still in wins, and all items are revealed sorted with a source.
BUT the core turn already exists in SMART10's "true/false" cards (Smart10, 2017, Arno Steinwender & Christoph Reiser;
Martinex, Piatnik, Bananagrams and many other publishers). There, ten items are shown to everyone; in turn each player
picks one item they think is "true"; lifting the marker checks it; right = keep it and the next player faces fewer
items; wrong = out of that card. The main differences: in Smart10 you may pass, a wrong answer only costs that round
(and its markers), the winner is the first to 30 points, it is general trivia with six question types, and there is
no bar country, no real figures and no sorted reveal.
Other relatives, from closest to farthest:
- Tenable board game (wrong answers eliminate, a top-ten list runs out; but you recall, you do not pick from a pool);
- Know It or Blow It (wrong = zero for the round, "Don't Know It!" to stop; recall, teams);
- Cardline: Globetrotter and GeoXpert (countries with hidden real statistics, placed in a line, harder as the line grows;
  no pool, no elimination);
- GeoCards World (guess which of two countries is bigger by size or population);
- The World Game and Das Laender-Quiz (Top Trumps-style comparing of country facts);
- Ticked Off (bid and write lists; far).

(2) The names as board or card game names:
- Last One Standing: USED by at least two board games: "Last One Standing: The Battle Royale Board Game" (2018,
  battle royale / fighting, Brendan McCaskell) and "Last One Standing" (2021, danspil, abstract pawn-movement game
  where "Once you cannot move, you are out"). Neither is a trivia game.
- Still In: no board or card game with this name found (1 search shared with Safe Pick).
- Sure Thing: no board or card game with this name found (1 search; the pages that came back do not contain the phrase).
- Safe Pick: no board or card game with this name found (1 search shared with Still In).
- Thin Ice: USED often: Pressman dexterity game (1989, wet marbles on a tissue), Looney Labs / Jacob Davenport
  Icehouse dexterity game (1997), "Thin Ice: Survival has never been so much fun" (party game on a melting ice sheet),
  "Angry Birds: On Thin Ice" board game. None is trivia.
- Close Call: USED by "Close Call" (12G Games on The Game Crafter, 1-2 players, air traffic control tile game). Not trivia.
