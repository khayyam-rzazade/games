ONE OF 193: EVIDENCE
====================
What each page said, for every list the phone answers from. Read on 5 October 2026 in the chat that built the game.
Four helpers did the reading in parallel (each with a fixed budget of web searches; every address opened came from
their own search results or was a link on the same website). Claude compared the lists with the files in
r/one-of-193-workshop/countries.py (a script mapped every name in these quotes to its country code: all lists match).

Parts
  A  the 193 UN member states, and the UN regions and sub-regions (M49)
  B  landlocked, island countries, the Mediterranean, the equator
  C  coasts on the Pacific, Indian, Atlantic and Arctic oceans: NOT used (the sources do not agree; see the verdict)
  D  land borders with China and with Russia, left-hand traffic, the euro

What the game took (C):
  - only UN member states: territories, observer states (the Holy See, Palestine), states in free association
    (the Cook Islands, Niue), Western Sahara and partially recognised states (Kosovo, Taiwan, Northern Cyprus,
    Abkhazia, South Ossetia, Transnistria) are left out;
  - each country's own land only: overseas territories never count (so the United Kingdom is not a
    Mediterranean country, though Gibraltar and Akrotiri and Dhekelia touch the sea);
  - the ocean questions are left out of the menu (part C).

The marks [T], [OBS], [FA], [DISP] and every "COMPARISON" or "NOTES" line are the helpers' own, not the pages'.

KEY TO MARKS USED IN THIS FILE (all marks are mine, not the pages')
======================================================================
(no mark) = UN member state (one of the 193 in List A1).
[T]    = NOT a UN member state: territory, dependency or other area.
         Based on List A1 (both A1 sources list the same 193 member states).
[OBS]  = NOT a UN member state: one of the "two UN observer states
         (the Holy See and Palestine)" (wording from the Wikipedia
         geoscheme list page).
[FA]   = NOT a UN member state: one of the "two states in free association
         with New Zealand (the Cook Islands and Niue)" (same page).
[DISP] = NOT a UN member state: Western Sahara, "a disputed territory whose
         sovereignty is contested" (same page).
Everything marked [T], [OBS], [FA] or [DISP] is to be left out of the
193-country game list.
[...] = a gap left by the page reader. No gaps remain in the final lists
(see Part 4, Notes).

"An island is a landmass (smaller than a continent) that is surrounded by water. Many island countries are spread over an archipelago, as is the case with Indonesia, Japan, and the Philippines—these countries consist of thousands of islands. Others consist of a single island, such as Barbados, Dominica, and Nauru; a main island and some smaller islands, such as Cuba, Iceland, and Sri Lanka; a part of an island, such as Brunei, the Dominican Republic, Timor-Leste, and the Republic of Ireland; or one main island but also sharing borders in other islands, such as the United Kingdom (Great Britain and a part of Ireland). In total, 50 island countries have been included in the lists. Australia is not included as it is considered a continental country, although it was historically referred to as an island country because of its lack of land borders. Greenland is generally considered as the largest island on Earth and listed among the island territories. Puerto Rico in the Caribbean Sea is officially an unincorporated territory of the United States. Neither Greenland nor Puerto Rico are sovereign countries. Indonesia is the world's largest island country by area (1,904,569 km2), and by total number of islands (17,504 islands). It is also the world's most populous island country, with a population of over 270 million (the fourth most populous country in the world, after India, China, and the United States). South America has only one independent sovereign island nation with Trinidad and Tobago; though considered a Caribbean island country, it is located on the northern portion of the South American continental shelf just 11 kilometres (6 nautical miles) off Venezuela, but 130 kilometres (70 nautical miles) from Grenada, the nearest of the Antilles."

- I found no reputable site with a complete list. Wikipedia's two Pacific lists name no American country, apart from Panama in prose. WorldAtlas has no Pacific list, only two-ocean and three-ocean lists. The only complete-looking lists were on small, unvetted sites.
- Disagreements found:
  (1) East Timor: in Wikipedia "Pacific coast" and both two-ocean tables, but missing from the Wikipedia "Pacific Ocean" list.
  (2) Argentina: Wikipedia's table puts the Beagle Channel in the Pacific column ("1, 2 or 3" oceans). WorldAtlas's table gives it no Pacific coast ("1 or 2").
  (3) Some Pacific coasts are on a marginal sea or gulf in the tables: Thailand (Gulf of Thailand), Malaysia (South China Sea), Honduras (Gulf of Fonseca), Panama (Gulf of Panama), East Timor (Savu Sea, Flores Sea). Those countries count only if these waters count as Pacific. Wikipedia and WorldAtlas both count them. Wikipedia also lists Singapore, Cambodia, Vietnam and Brunei as Pacific, but no WorldAtlas page I opened mentions them. [my note] Which water gives those four their coast is not stated on any page I opened.
  (4) Ecuador, Peru and El Salvador are never named as Pacific-coast countries on any reputable page I opened.

C2 INDIAN: NO.
- No reputable page I opened lists the countries. Wikipedia (infobox), Britannica and WorldAtlas all describe the ocean by regions. Britannica names only Iran, Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Australia.
- The definition itself has changed, according to Wikipedia: the IHO's 1953 limits excluded the northern marginal seas, and its 2002 limits included them. So Red Sea and Persian Gulf states depend on the definition. Wikipedia's area figure, WorldAtlas and Nations Online count those seas in; Britannica is silent.
- East Timor (Timor Sea) is Indian Ocean in Wikipedia's and WorldAtlas's tables.

C3 ATLANTIC: NO.
- I found no complete list. The Wikipedia list, as read, is clearly incomplete: no mainland American state, no Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Poland, Sweden, Finland or the Baltic states, no Algeria, Tunisia, Libya or Egypt. WorldAtlas has no Atlantic list.
- Disagreements on marginal seas:
  * Wikipedia counts the North, Baltic, Mediterranean and Black Seas as Atlantic, but notes that the IHO limits are "not recognized by various authorities".
  * WorldAtlas counts the Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico, Baltic, Black Sea and Mediterranean as Atlantic.
  * Nations Online calls the Mediterranean only "connected with" the Atlantic.
  * WorldAtlas puts the Norwegian Sea, Greenland Sea and Hudson Bay in BOTH the Atlantic and the Arctic, on different pages.
  * Overseas territories (France, UK, Netherlands, US, Denmark) are listed separately by Wikipedia and counted only conditionally by WorldAtlas.

C4 ARCTIC: YES for the two main list sources, but NOT FULLY CLEAR.
- Wikipedia "Arctic Ocean" and WorldAtlas "Countries With Arctic Ocean Coastlines" agree exactly. By own land the UN members are Canada, Iceland, Norway, Russia and the United States. Greenland (Denmark) is a territory. Britannica Kids and WorldAtlas "Arctic Ocean" give the same six names.
- BUT another reputable page, Wikipedia "Arctic Five", says "Iceland is generally not regarded as an Arctic Ocean littoral State", and counts the "Danish Realm" via Greenland. Wikipedia's own two-ocean table hedges Iceland as "1 or 2" oceans.
- So:
  * Canada, Norway, Russia and the United States are agreed by every page I read.
  * Iceland is contested.
  * Denmark counts only through Greenland. That makes it a "no" under the brief's own-land rule, but a "yes" in the "Arctic Five" sense.
