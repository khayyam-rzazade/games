## 3. Overall verdict

**I found no app on the Apple App Store or Google Play that is the same game.** I found nothing where the device hides a real country, players pick yes/no questions from a fixed menu, the device answers from real data, and a count of remaining countries is shown.

The closest relatives:
1. **Reverse games: Wind of Earth and Guess Countries** (both by Mikolaj Adamowicz). They use real statistics, about 193 to 200 countries, and show elimination visually, but the app asks and the player hides the country.
2. **General AI 20-questions apps where players ask the device:** 2Q (RoxyKovu, with pass-and-play and Apple TV groups), AI 20 Questions (BLUETAG, "Places & Landmarks"), Guessio ("Person, Place, or Thing") and Guesso. None is about countries specifically. In all of them, questions are typed freely and answered by an AI, not picked from a menu and answered from checked data. None shows a count of remaining candidates.
3. **Real-data hidden-country guessing games with hints or feedback:** Country Quiz: Geography Guess (Motecso), MapGame!, Guess_The_Country, Worldle and Globle. Players guess countries and get clues back; they never ask questions.

**I found no listing that shows players a live count of countries still possible.** The nearest is the reverse apps' vanishing flags or map.

**Name warning:** "Find The Country" is already the name of an App Store app (GV Software Solutions Limited, a map-tapping quiz).

**One lead outside my channel:** guessthatcountry.com is a two-player web game where players ask yes/no questions about a randomly assigned real country. From the page, I could not confirm who answers or whether questions come from a list. It is worth a direct look.

**What I could not check:**
- Two 20-questions listings returned 404 (20Questions.AI on Apple; Twenty Questions - AI Powered on Google Play).
- ClueClimb was behind a login.
- I read Guess Countries through a listing site, not its Apple page.
- I did not open the second Wind of Earth listing (id1498348654), the Motecso Apple pages, the extra Globle Play listings, or about 20 plain-quiz listings.
- Search engines index store pages unevenly, and the search tool is US-only.
- Store descriptions often leave out features like a candidate counter.

So this is "nothing found in 12 searches", not proof that nothing exists.

## 3. Overall verdict

**No exact match found on the web.** Nothing I found combines all of these: the phone hides a country, players pick yes/no questions from a menu, answers come from checked data, a live count of countries still possible, group play on one phone, a wrong-guess penalty, and a reveal of how few questions would have been enough.

But the core loop does exist, in small or partial forms:
- **Twenty Questions Geography** (Hugging Face) is the core loop in code: the computer hides a country, the player types yes/no questions, and an AI answers. It is a small demo with no checked data, no menu and no count.
- **Guess That Country** (guessthatcountry.com) is a two-player online game where players ask about each other's assigned country. The answers appear to come from the opponent, not from data.
- Human versions are old and common: 20 questions, the Guess in 10 card game, Mystery Skype, ESL cards. So the basic idea is not new. What would be new is the combination: a question menu, real-data answers, the live count, group play on one phone, and the "fewest questions" reveal.

The **"countries still possible" display** only turned up in reverse (the Guess Countries iOS app, where flags vanish from a globe). I found no web game that shows a number of remaining countries. I also found nothing that shows "how few questions would have been enough".

The nearest popular relatives are the **attribute-feedback dailies** (Countryle, Geodle, Country Guessr, customwordle.io). They differ because you guess countries and get graded, instead of asking questions.

**No SAME game found in the board and card channel.**

- The basic idea of guessing a hidden real country with yes/no questions is **not new** as a board/card game. Skillmatics' **Guess in 10: Countries of the World** does it with a hidden card and a 10-question budget.   1. the device answers from real data, not a person;
  2. a fixed menu of questions;
  3. a live count of countries still possible;
  4. a reveal of the fewest questions that would have been enough, plus sourced facts;
  5. one shared phone for a group or one player.

  Every close relative has a person answering free-form questions (Guess in 10; guessthatcountry.com, by the look of it) or uses fixed clues instead of questions. I found no board, card or party game that counts remaining candidates.
- **Name clash:** Mappr already uses "Find the Country" for a map quiz.
