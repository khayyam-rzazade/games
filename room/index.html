Rooms

A room is a group of friends — 2 to 50 — playing the same small daily games. One link makes you a member; after that, every daily game you finish joins the room's table by itself: today's standings and the month's.

The link is the key

There is no login and no password. The room's code is 20 letters and digits drawn at random — far too many to guess — and it rides in the part of the link after the #, which browsers never send to other sites. Whoever has the link is in, so share it only with the friends you mean.

Your name

Nobody is given a name here. The first time one is needed, a window asks what your friends call you — the same name you may already have set for Who Was Closer. It is kept in this browser, and in a room it is sent with your scores, so the table can say who took what. Two members of one room cannot share a name.

Who takes a game, and the month

A game is taken by whoever was strictly closest that day, by the game's own rule — The Club by most right, and Your Call is never a win, as in Who Was Closer. A tie takes nothing, and a game only you played takes nothing. A point per game taken; the month is the sum of its days, and it starts fresh on the 1st.

What is sent, and what is kept

When you finish a daily game, your phone sends the game, your score, the day and your name to the rooms you are in — nothing else, nothing of any answer, and nothing at all if you are in no room. It goes to the site's own small server and nowhere further. The server keeps this month and the last, then deletes; a room nobody has played in for 60 days is deleted whole, and no address, email or cookie is stored for anyone. Leaving a room deletes everything of yours at once.

Leaving, and deleting a room

Leave the room, on the table below, deletes you and every score of yours from the room at once. Whoever made the room can delete the whole room the same way. If anything is ever stuck, write to the contact address and it will be deleted by hand.

Your streak and your albums

A room never touches them. The games are scored, kept and shown exactly as always; the room only hears about results you were keeping anyway.
