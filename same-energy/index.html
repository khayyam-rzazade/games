    How to play

    1. One real food or drink a day at the top. Below it, a plate or glasses to fill with something else.
    2. Drag up on the plate, or tap it, to add; drag down to take away. Stop when you think it holds the same energy as the thing at the top.
    3. Tap Show the real amount. The real amount fills in over yours, and yellow marks the part you got wrong.

    One day a day, the same for everyone. A new one arrives at midnight.

    Where the figures come from

    The energy of every food and drink was read in two official food tables: FoodData Central, of the United States Department of Agriculture, and Matvaretabellen, Norway's national food table. The two agree within about a tenth, and the answer comes out the same from either, rounded to half a portion. Both are linked under the answer. The game counts food and glasses; it never shows a figure for the energy itself.

    The portions: an apple is 180 g, a banana 120 g without its skin, a slice of bread 30 g, a boiled egg 50 g and a grape 5 g; a glass holds 250 ml and a can 330 ml. With grapes, within a tenth of the answer counts as spot on.

    Why a drink is not the same as a plate: in studies, people who had extra energy as a soft drink went on eating as much as before, while the same energy as solid food was made up for later in the day (DiMeglio and Mattes, 2000).

    Someone to talk to

    If food or eating is hard for you, you are not alone. These help lines are free and open on weekdays: Beat in the United Kingdom (0808 801 0677; in Scotland 0808 801 0432, in Wales 0808 801 0433, in Northern Ireland 0808 801 0434), the National Alliance for Eating Disorders in the United States (+1 866 662 1235), and SOS Disturbi Alimentari in Italy (800 180 969).
