Para quem pesquisa

Cada pergunta de Descubra quem você é vem de uma escala de pesquisa publicada e é mostrada palavra por palavra, com sua escala de resposta publicada. O que é nosso (os nomes, as instruções de uma linha, algumas frases de preenchimento, os nomes dos países) está marcado. Cada pequena mudança feita para o celular está na tabela de desvios. As pontuações são calculadas pelas regras abaixo, o mesmo código no celular e no servidor. As respostas ficam guardadas só de quem diz sim no fim, sem nome, e-mail ou endereço; os dados anônimos serão publicados depois do primeiro artigo, com menos detalhe, para que ninguém possa ser reconhecido. Find out about yourself — item sheet, Brazilian Portuguese (version 2, 27 October 2026; item_version 40)

Version 1 (earlier today) was written from what the web gave. Version 2 adds three files obtained (the ESS10 Portugal questionnaire, the YouGov Brazil 2023 questionnaire, the ISSP 2013 basic questionnaire) and a second search for sources written in Portuguese and actually fielded, then adapted under the project's principles. item_version stays 40: nothing has been pushed.

44 of the 57 scored items are published Portuguese: - 33 are Brazilian. - 11 are Portugal's, in the instruments' own Portuguese (ESS, ISSP, Eurobarometer). - 38 of the 44 are word for word, apart from the country's name and the article an item takes when it is listed on its own. 6 carry a small documented change.

The rest: - The four riddles come from a Brazilian fielding (a 2022 USP dissertation), adapted. - Nine items are our translation, back-translated: - the five conspiracy items (no Portuguese CMQ printing its items exists) - two child-rearing pairs - one ISSP item whose published Portuguese asks a different question - the risk dial

Version 1 had 26 published items.

The Portuguese companion to the English item sheet (English v1.1), built like the Spanish item sheet (v4): the same nine games, the same scoring, the same bands (their Portuguese names come from the copy doc, left blank here), the same conventions on screen (one item per screen, Skip, Back, the readout "4 de 5"). Only the words change. Everything in the English sheet's rules holds unless this file says otherwise.

How each text was chosen (the order for Portuguese): 1. a Brazilian published version — a Brazilian validation article or a Brazilian questionnaire that was fielded; 2. where only Portugal's exists, Portugal's, changed only in spelling or where a Brazilian reader would trip, each change in the Deviations table; 3. our translation, back-translated, marked "our translation, back-translated, not validated". The rule: look for texts written in the language and used, not for translations made for us, and adapt them; keep it simple. Nothing is quoted from memory or from a search snippet.

Principle 15 decides between published versions. A published item yields when it is a different item or names a different object. Translation variation inside the right item stays. In this sheet it bit five times, each explained where it falls: - the Brazilian "O Poder Judiciário" (B7) - Portugal's ISSP Q3b ("ashamed of being Portuguese") - the ESEB and LAPOP interest and satisfaction items (other surveys' scales) - LAPOP's and the Brazil 2023 creativity pair - Andrade's BFI items 2 and 4

How the texts were read. No text was retyped from a summary. - Brazil 2023 questionnaire: by unpacking the .docx and reading its XML. - ESS10 Portugal: with pdftotext. - Bandeira et al., Roiz Junior et al. and the ELSA-Brasil article: as page text in the browser. - Andrade (2008) and dos Santos (2022): from the PDFs' text layer, read with pdf.js in the browser. - Global Flourishing Study translations: from the spreadsheet's own data on OSF. - Portugal's ISSP 2013 and Eurobarometer 85.2 questionnaires: as extracted text, each line asked for twice with the same result.

Which Portuguese. The site's Portuguese is Brazilian. Brazilian published items are used as printed. Portugal's items are used only where no Brazilian version of the same item exists, and are changed only where a Brazilian reader would trip. Two European turns are kept and flagged, because a Brazilian reads them without trouble: - "todo o cuidado é pouco" (dial 4) - "está a caminhar" (dial 9)

Our own translations use Brazilian words that a reader in Portugal, Angola or Mozambique also understands.

Register. The Brazil 2023 questionnaire, the GFS and the dos Santos riddles use "você", the register of Brazilian web surveys, and so do our translations and the "?" texts. The other sources keep their own form of address: - ELSA-Brasil addresses the respondent as "o(a) senhor(a)". - Portugal's ESS and ISSP and Bandeira's instruction use the formal third person ("acha", "concorda"). - The Eurobarometer uses "diria".

In Brazil both forms take the third-person verb, so the mix reads naturally. Published items keep their form of address (D37 PT). The on-screen lines around the items are the copy doc's ("você").

Gender. Where Portuguese would give the player a gender, the sources' own Brazilian convention is used — "(a)", as in Brazil 2023's "satisfeito(a)" and "cidadão(ã)" — and every "(a)" we add is recorded (D38 PT). The Brazilian BFI is built on "Eu me considero uma pessoa que…", so its adjectives agree with "pessoa" ("É reservada") and fit everyone.

Scoring (published): reverse 1, 3, 4, 5, 7 (6 − x); each trait = mean of its two items.

Why Roiz Junior's items and not Andrade's. Andrade's IGFP-5 is the more widely used version, but two of its BFI-10 items fail principle 15: - Item 2: "É geralmente confiável" means is reliable, trustworthy, not is generally trusting (of others). - Item 4: "É relaxado, controla bem o estresse" — in Brazil "relaxado" also means sloppy, a Conscientiousness word inside a Neuroticism item.

Roiz Junior's renderings of both ("Geralmente confia, acredita nos outros"; "É tranquila, lida bem com estresse") have neither problem. The other eight are close in both versions. Andrade's stem ("Eu me vejo como alguém que ...") is the English stem word for word, but its items take the masculine; Roiz Junior's stem and items go together, and their feminine agreement with "pessoa" needs no slash. So: Roiz Junior's stem and items, Andrade's scale and instruction.

Caveat for the paper (opened). The short forms' structure is weak in Brazil: - Roiz Junior et al.'s best model keeps 25 items and drops six of the ten BFI-10 items. - Mastrascusa et al. did not test the BFI-10's own structure because its items loaded low.

This is a known limit of the BFI-10 in many countries; it goes in the paper.

  • POP1. The published item says "Os congressistas" — the members of Brazil's Congress — which is right for a Brazilian and wrong for a player from Portugal or Angola. "Os parlamentares" is the everyday Brazilian word for members of any parliament (and for Brazil's own), so one word makes the item fit every country without a placeholder, as the Spanish "Los parlamentarios" does. The English D6 (localising POP1) does not apply. Second option: a placeholder, "Os congressistas" for Brazilian players only and "Os parlamentares" for everyone else.
  • POP7, translation variation, kept. "consenso" (consensus) for "compromise" and "abrir mão de seus princípios" (giving up one's principles) for "selling out" are looser than the English, and "what people call" is dropped. The Spanish team's own 2023 Spain questionnaire has the same "consenso" rendering. It is the same proposition — that reaching agreement across sides betrays principle — so it stays. Mixing a closer item from another fielding into a one-source battery would cost more in invariance than the paraphrase does (the Spanish sheet's game 8 rule). Second option: Portugal's ICS/ISCTE 2019 wording (P6.6, opened in version 1), "Em política, aquilo que se chama 'chegar a um compromisso' significa na verdade abdicar dos próprios princípios" — nearer the English, from another fielding.

The source's wording (Apêndice), and what was changed: 1. "Se você está em uma corrida de carros e ultrapassa…" — "de carros" (car race) removed: the English is a race on foot, and "corrida" alone covers both. 2. "Um fazendeiro tem 15 ovelhas e todas, exceto 8, morreram. Quantas sobraram?" — "tem" (has) → "tinha" (had), as the English and the dissertation's own Tabela 4 ("tinha"); "sobraram" (are left) kept from the Apêndice. 3. "O pai de João tem três filhos. Os dois primeiros se chamam Abril e Maio. Qual o nome do terceiro?" — the dissertation made the children boys, which leaves two boys called April and May. We keep the English daughters and the dissertation's sentence shape ("As duas primeiras se chamam Abril e Maio"), as in its own Tabela 4 ("O pai de Emily tem três filhas…"). The name is ours: Laura, a common Brazilian girl's name that is not a month and does not echo one ("Júlia" would echo "Julho", one of the wrong options; names in "Ma-" echo "Maio" and "Março"). The months stay, as in English and Spanish: the lure works because the reader runs on from Abril and Maio to Junho. 4. "…3 metros de profundidade x 3 metros de largura x 3 metros de comprimento?" — the "x" written as commas and "e", as in the English sheet.

Second option for riddle 3: "Abril e Maia" (a real name echoing Maio) — weaker trap. We recommend the months. Riddle 4: metres, as in English (D15). After the four (as the copy doc has it): Você já tinha visto alguma destas charadas? Sim · Não.

Game 7 — "Who do you trust?" · Trust in institutions · ESS

All seven institutions are published Portuguese.

Sources (opened, files). - Brazil 2023, Q15 (rows Q15_111 to Q15_115): the ESS institutional-trust question asked in Brazilian Portuguese, online.
