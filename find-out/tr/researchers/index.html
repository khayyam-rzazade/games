Riddle 1 as printed: "…geçseydeniz…", a typo for "geçseydiniz", corrected. Answers as printed: "İkinci" (analytic), "Birinci" (intuitive).

Back-translations: 1 If you were running in a race and you passed the person in 2nd place, what place would you be in? · 2 A farmer had 15 sheep and all of them except 8 died. How many sheep are left? · 3 Elif's father has three daughters. The first two are named Nisan (April) and Mayıs (May). What is the third daughter's name? · 4 How many cubic metres of earth are there in a hole that is 3 m deep, 3 m wide and 3 m long? Options: Second · First · Third · Last; 8 · 7 · 15 · 0; Elif · Haziran (June) · Temmuz (July) · Mart (March); None · 27 · 9 · 3.

The names riddle. The lure must be the next month, as June is in English. Turkish months are Ocak, Şubat, Mart, Nisan, Mayıs, Haziran, Temmuz… "Nisan" is a common Turkish girl's name; "Mayıs" and "Haziran" are not names, but the riddle needs only the run of months, and the run is what pulls the quick answer (the English pair "April and May" works the same way). The other wrong options keep the English logic: the month after the lure (Temmuz) and the one before the first (Mart). "Elif" is one of the most common Turkish girls' names, as Emily is in English; Şahin kept the English "Mary" in his names riddle, but a Turkish name makes the trap fairer. Another option considered, using real names only: seasons, "İlk ikisinin adı Bahar ve Yaz" (Spring, Summer — both real names) → lure "Güz" (Autumn), others "Kış" · "Nisan"; weaker because the run has only four steps.

After the four — OURS: Bu bilmecelerden herhangi birini daha önce görmüş müydünüz? · Evet · Hayır. (Had you seen any of these riddles before? Yes · No.) Explanations on the reveal: in the copy doc. Scoring: as in English.

Item 1 (published since 24 October). The same document's English version prints the same question as item 32, p. 10: "Would you describe yourself as someone who tries to avoid risks (risk-averse) or as someone who is willing to take risks (risk-prone)?", ends Risk-averse · Risk-prone. The Turkish follows the German original's order and its opening clause ("Wie schätzen Sie sich persönlich ein:" → "Kendinizi şahsen nasıl değerlendiriyorsunuz:"), which the English drops; kept as printed. Its own instruction line, not shown on screen (D20), reads: "Lütfen 0 değerinin "rizikoya hiç girmem" anlamına ve 10 değerinin "hep rizikoya girerim" anlamına geldiği bir skalada bir değer belirtin. Aradaki değerler yardımıyla görüşünüzü derecelendirebilirsiniz."

The word riziko (kept, 26 October). The published Turkish says riziko (and rizikoya girmek, to take a risk), not risk. The document is the SOEP's translation aid for Turkish speakers in Germany, and it takes the German Risiko. It is a real Turkish word and this is the fielded wording, so by this sheet's own rule it is kept — and it makes Turkish answers comparable with the SOEP's own Turkish fieldwork. The lines around the item (the copy doc: the dial's name, the comparison line) stay risk, the word a reader in Türkiye would write today; a player reads "risk" in the frame and "riziko" in the published question. Our translation of 23 October, for the record: Genel olarak, risk almaya tamamen hazır biri misiniz, yoksa risk almaktan kaçınmaya mı çalışırsınız?, ends Risk almaya hiç istekli değilim · Risk almaya tamamen hazırım (back-translation: Generally, are you someone who is fully ready to take risks, or do you try to avoid taking risks?).
