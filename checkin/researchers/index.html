Every question in the Anonymous Check-in comes from published research, and this page shows how. The table below marks each question as verbatim (the published words and answer scale), adapted (our short version of a published survey item, with what was changed) or descriptive (ours, used only to describe, never in a score). The wording of every question in every language comes from the same sources; where no published translation could be found, the translation is our own and not validated. Outside English, published questions keep the form of address they were published in, and our own translations of them use the language's usual survey form (Lei, usted, você, vous, siz), as in Find out who you are. The words we wrote ourselves use the informal you, as everywhere on Logicers.

How the result is worked out

The five WHO-5 answers (0 to 5 each) are added up and multiplied by 4, which gives 0 to 100, as the instrument's authors publish it. The result word comes from four bands: 0–28, 29–50, 51–72 and 73–100. In clinical research a score of 50 or less is the recommended cut-off when the WHO-5 is used to screen for depression, and 28 or less is a stricter cut, near the level found in major depression; in Danish population studies the average is 70 (Topp et al., 2015). The split at 72 is ours, so that the upper half has two words. The check-in screens no one: it shows a number and a word about the last two weeks, never a diagnosis, and a low number never reaches the host. The six why-questions, the three adapted statements, the left-out question and sleep each become a bar from 0 to 100, where 100 lifts and 0 weighs; the formulas are in the codebook below. The bar that weighs most picks one line of advice: the lowest bar under 50, a tie going to the earlier question; with no bar under 50, a line that says to keep going.

From 0 to 28, the result first says that the last two weeks have been hard, suggests talking to someone the person trusts, links to free helplines in their country (Find A Helpline, findahelpline.com) and says to call the local emergency number in danger. This shows on the person's phone only.

Where the advice comes from

The player's line comes from Five Ways to Wellbeing (Aked et al., 2008, the evidence review for the UK Government's Foresight project), the WHO guidelines on physical activity (2020), and sleep research (Hirshkowitz et al., 2015; Sletten et al., 2023).

The host's line

The host's line comes from the Job Demands–Resources model (Bakker & Demerouti, 2007) and the UK Health and Safety Executive's Management Standards for work-related stress. It is picked by the group's lowest of the six why-bars under 50, from 10 phones; sleep and the life-worded bars never pick it, because no host can change them. Under that line the host reads: "Ask your group first. They usually know what would help." (HSE, 2009, p. 4: check the results with the people concerned, as they are closest to the issues). "We'll try this" and the next round follow HSE's steps 4 and 5: decide how you will know an action works, then watch it.

What is kept

Every check-in adds to an anonymous count per day and country (WHO-5 sums and stress answers as counts, no code), which draws the world and country curves, and to a count per day of how many started, finished, said yes or no. Nothing else is kept unless the player says yes at the end. Then one row per check-in: a random code made in the browser, the room (as a one-way hash, or none), the round, the language, the item version, the role, the age group and country if given, the 20 answers, the day and the version of the consent text. No time of day, no name, no email, no IP address. Answers wait on the server for up to 24 hours for the yes, with the time they arrived; a no deletes them at once, and so does silence after 24 hours (the sweep runs every hour). "Delete my answers" removes a kept row at any time. The host's page keeps only group totals per round, never a row per person, and is deleted 2 months after its last use.

People under 18 can take part and say yes like anyone else: they should be heard too. Their answers are studied as a group of their own.

Groups

A group's charts open at 10 people, each side of a split at 10, and a country at 30. Group results appear only after the reveal (by the host, or by itself at the end of the round), so the timing of an answer never points to a person. A host cannot see or export anyone's answers. One thing the floor of 10 cannot stop: a host who answers nine times on phones of their own in a round of ten could work out the tenth person's answers.

The study

What explains differences in wellbeing and stress between groups and countries. It is exploratory, with no hypotheses registered in advance. For people who said yes both here and in Find out who you are, the two sets of answers can be joined through the same random code; nothing else joins them. The anonymous data will be published after the first paper, coarsened so that no one can be recognised. The codebook below lists every variable, value and rule.

A bar above 50 shows "lifts you", below 50 "weighs on you", at 50 "in between". The ladder and "on my mind" have no bar, and stress is shown as its own number. The why-bars are 0–100 the COPSOQ way, turned so that 100 always lifts: a COPSOQ scale score for demands or insecurity is 100 − bar. A change of 10 WHO-5 points or more is the review's threshold for a clinically relevant change (Topp et al., 2015, p. 170): a yardstick for rounds and repeat check-ins, not a test. Group values are means over the phones that answered each question, shown from 10.

Scope

#Rule
S1Kept rows only (the player said yes). Waiting rows never reach the export.
S2A cross-sectional analysis uses one row per id, the first kept one. Change over time uses all rows of an id in day order.
S3A country table needs country ≠ not said and at least 30 rows.
S4Under-18s are kept and counted as a group of their own.
S5A room is a cluster, not a sample: report with and without rooms, or model the room.
S6role changes the words of the six why-questions: compare across roles only after a test of measurement invariance, or report per role.
S7Languages are pooled only after the same check; several translations are our own and not validated.
S8Joined with Find out who you are only for an id with a yes in both; the joined file is never published with id.
S9Rows flagged C1 or C6 leave every analysis; C2 and C3 are reported with and without.

Known limits

Self-selected players; in rooms, selected by whoever hosts. Not a sample of any country. One check-in is a two-week window, and the stress item a single question. The three adapted statements are short versions of published items, not the published scales. Sleep is one night; "on my mind" is one tap. A phone that clears its storage gets a new code, so repeats are counted low.
